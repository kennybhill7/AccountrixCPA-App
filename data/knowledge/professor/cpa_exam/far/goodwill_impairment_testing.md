# Goodwill Impairment Testing - ASC 350

## Goodwill Characteristics
- **Nature**: Intangible asset arising from business combination
- **NOT amortized** (exception: private company may elect amortization)
- **Tested for impairment**: At least annually + when triggering events occur
- **Unit of account**: Reporting unit (operating segment or one level below)

## Impairment Testing Approach

### Two-Step Option
1. **Step 0** (Optional): Qualitative assessment
2. **Step 1**: Quantitative test if necessary

### Timing
- At least **annually**
- More frequently if triggering events occur

## Step 0: Qualitative Assessment (Optional)

### Purpose
Determine whether quantitative test is necessary

### Question
Is it **more likely than not (>50%)** that FV of reporting unit < carrying amount?

### Decision
- **Yes** → Perform quantitative test
- **No** → No further testing required (no impairment)

### Factors to Consider
- Macroeconomic conditions
- Industry and market considerations
- Cost factors affecting performance
- Overall financial performance
- Entity-specific events (management/strategy changes)
- Sustained decrease in share price (public companies)
- Impairment of assets within reporting unit

### Benefit
May skip costly quantitative test if clearly no impairment

## Step 1: Quantitative Test

### Process
Compare **FV of reporting unit** to its **carrying amount** (including goodwill)

### Comparison
- **FV ≥ Carrying amount** → No impairment
- **FV < Carrying amount** → Impairment exists

### Impairment Loss Calculation
```
Impairment Loss = Carrying Amount - Fair Value
```

**Limited to**: Carrying amount of goodwill (cannot write down below zero)

### Recognition
```
DR Impairment Loss - Goodwill
   CR Goodwill
```

Recognize in income (operating expense)

### Reversal Rule
Goodwill impairment losses **CANNOT be reversed**

## Calculation Example

### Facts
**Reporting Unit**:
- Net assets (excluding goodwill): $800,000
- Goodwill: $200,000
- **Total carrying amount**: $1,000,000

**Fair value of reporting unit**: $900,000

### Analysis

**Step 1: Compare FV to Carrying Amount**
```
Fair Value:           $900,000
Carrying Amount:    $1,000,000
────────────────────────────────
FV < CA → Impairment exists
```

**Step 2: Calculate Impairment Loss**
```
Carrying Amount     $1,000,000
Fair Value            (900,000)
────────────────────────────────
Impairment Loss       $100,000
```

Check: Limited to goodwill balance ($200,000)
Since $100,000 < $200,000 → Recognize full $100,000

### Journal Entry
```
DR Impairment Loss - Goodwill    $100,000
   CR Goodwill                            $100,000
```

### New Carrying Amount
```
Goodwill: $200,000 - $100,000 = $100,000
```
(New basis, not reversed even if FV recovers)

## Triggering Events

### Examples Requiring Interim Testing
- Significant adverse change in business climate
- Unanticipated competition or loss of key personnel
- Disposal or discontinuation of operations
- Impairment of significant asset group within reporting unit
- Loss of major customer or contract
- Significant decline in stock price (public companies)

## Private Company Alternative

### Election Available
Private companies may elect to:
1. **Amortize goodwill** on straight-line basis over 10 years (or less if shorter useful life)
2. **Perform impairment testing at entity level** (instead of reporting unit level)

### Benefits
- Simplified accounting
- Reduced costs

### Trade-Off
Reduces comparability with public companies

## Comparison to Long-Lived Assets

| Aspect | Goodwill (ASC 350) | Long-Lived Assets (ASC 360) |
|--------|-------------------|---------------------------|
| **Amortization** | NO (unless private co. elects) | YES (depreciated/amortized) |
| **Impairment test** | FV vs. CA (one-step) | Recoverability, then FV (two-step) |
| **Impairment indicator** | Annual + triggering events | Triggering events only |
| **Reversal allowed** | NO | NO |
| **Measurement** | Reporting unit level | Asset or asset group level |

## Reporting Unit Determination

### Definition
Operating segment or one level below (component)

### Component is Reporting Unit If:
- Constitutes a business
- Discrete financial information available
- Segment management regularly reviews operating results

### Aggregation
Components with similar economic characteristics may be aggregated

## Goodwill Allocation

### Principle
Allocate goodwill to reporting units that benefit from acquisition synergies

### Method
Based on relative fair values of reporting units at acquisition date

### Note
Once allocated, goodwill remains with reporting unit (unless disposed)

## CPA Exam Tips

1. **Goodwill NOT amortized**, tested for impairment **annually** + when triggering events occur

2. **Optional qualitative assessment**: Skip quantitative if >50% likely FV ≥ Carrying amount

3. **Quantitative test**: FV of reporting unit < Carrying amount = Impairment

4. **Impairment loss** = Carrying amount - FV (limited to goodwill balance)

5. **Recognize impairment** in income, **CANNOT reverse**

6. **Private companies** may elect to amortize over 10 years

7. **Testing at reporting unit level** (not individual asset level)

## CPA Exam Strategies

### Testing Sequence
1. Optional qualitative first
2. If more likely than not impaired (>50%) → Do quantitative

### Quantitative Test
Simple comparison: FV < Carrying amount?
- If yes → Impairment = Difference (limited to goodwill balance)

### Key Difference
- **Goodwill**: One-step test (FV vs. CA)
- **Long-lived assets**: Two-step test (recoverability, then FV)

### No Reversal
Goodwill impairment **NEVER reversed** (unlike some other assets under IFRS)

### Annual Requirement
Goodwill tested **at least annually** (long-lived assets only when indicators present)

## Summary

### Key Takeaways
- Goodwill NOT amortized, tested for impairment annually + when triggering events occur
- Optional qualitative assessment: Skip quantitative if >50% likely FV ≥ Carrying amount
- Quantitative test: FV of reporting unit < Carrying amount = Impairment
- Impairment loss = Carrying amount - FV (limited to goodwill balance)
- Recognize impairment in income, **CANNOT reverse**
- Private companies may elect to amortize over 10 years
- Testing at reporting unit level (not individual asset level)
