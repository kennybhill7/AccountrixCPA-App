# Capital Structure Analysis and Presentation

## Overview
Capital structure refers to how a company finances its operations and growth through different sources of funds - primarily debt and equity.

---

## Components of Capital Structure

### Debt
- Bonds payable
- Notes payable
- Bank loans
- Finance leases

### Equity
- Common stock
- Preferred stock (non-redeemable)
- Additional paid-in capital
- Retained earnings
- Accumulated OCI
- Treasury stock (contra-equity)

### Hybrid Instruments
- Convertible bonds
- Redeemable preferred stock
- Stock warrants
- Stock options

---

## Simple vs. Complex Capital Structure

### Simple Capital Structure

**Definition**: Only common stock (no potentially dilutive securities)

**EPS Reporting**: Report **only Basic EPS**

**Characteristics**:
- No convertible securities
- No stock options or warrants
- No convertible preferred stock

### Complex Capital Structure

**Definition**: Has potentially dilutive securities

**Potentially Dilutive Securities**:
- Stock options
- Stock warrants
- Convertible bonds
- Convertible preferred stock

**EPS Reporting**: Report **both Basic AND Diluted EPS**

## Stockholders' Equity Presentation

### Standard Order on Balance Sheet

```
STOCKHOLDERS' EQUITY

Contributed Capital:
  Preferred stock, $10 par, 10,000 shares
    authorized, 5,000 issued and outstanding        $  50,000
  Common stock, $1 par, 100,000 shares authorized,
    50,000 issued, 48,000 outstanding                  50,000
  Additional paid-in capital                          200,000
    Total contributed capital                         300,000

Retained earnings                                     300,000
Accumulated other comprehensive income                 20,000

                                                      620,000
Less: Treasury stock, 2,000 shares at cost            (30,000)
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Total stockholders' equity                          $ 590,000
```

### Key Presentation Points

1. **Preferred stock** before common stock
2. **Additional paid-in capital** after stock accounts
3. **Retained earnings** after contributed capital
4. **Accumulated OCI** after retained earnings
5. **Treasury stock** as last item (contra-equity, reduction)

### Alternative Formats
May present:
- Contributed capital, retained earnings, and AOCI separately
- Or combined in various formats
- As long as all required components are disclosed

## Equity Disclosures Required

### For Each Class of Stock
- Number of shares **authorized**
- Number of shares **issued**
- Number of shares **outstanding**
- **Par value** per share

### Additional Disclosures
- Preferred stock preferences:
  - Dividend rates and terms
  - Liquidation preferences
  - Conversion features
- Treasury stock transactions
- Restrictions on retained earnings
- Stock option and warrant activity

## Debt vs. Equity Classification

### Debt Characteristics
- Fixed maturity date
- Mandatory payments
- Legal obligation to repay
- Not ownership interest
- Interest is deductible expense

### Equity Characteristics
- No maturity date
- Discretionary dividends
- Residual interest in assets
- Ownership in company
- Dividends not tax-deductible

### Requires Judgment
Some instruments require analysis per ASC 480:
- Mandatorily redeemable preferred stock
- Certain warrants
- Obligations to repurchase shares

## Book Value Per Share

### Calculation
```
Book Value per Share = (Total Stockholders' Equity - Preferred Equity)
                       ─────────────────────────────────────────────
                           Common Shares Outstanding
```

### Purpose
Indicates net asset value attributable to each common share

### Example
- Total stockholders' equity: $1,000,000
- Preferred stock equity: $100,000
- Common shares outstanding: 50,000

```
Book Value per Share = ($1,000,000 - $100,000) / 50,000
                     = $900,000 / 50,000
                     = $18 per share
```

## Contributed Capital vs. Retained Earnings

### Contributed Capital
**Source**: Capital from shareholders
- Stock issuances
- Additional paid-in capital
- Amounts received from owners

### Retained Earnings
**Source**: Capital from operations
- Cumulative net income
- Less: Cumulative dividends
- Amounts earned through business activities

### Legal Capital
**Definition**: Par value of issued shares
- Minimum capital required by state law in some jurisdictions
- Cannot be distributed as dividends in certain states

---

## Treasury Stock Impact

### Presentation
**Contra-equity** (reduction to total stockholders' equity)

### Shares Impact
- **Reduces** outstanding shares
- Does **NOT reduce** issued shares

### Cost Method (Most Common)
Record at cost paid to reacquire

**Entry at Purchase**:
```
DR Treasury Stock (at cost)
   CR Cash
```

**Entry at Reissuance**:
```
Above cost:
DR Cash
   CR Treasury Stock (at cost)
   CR APIC - Treasury Stock

Below cost:
DR Cash
DR APIC - Treasury Stock (or RE)
   CR Treasury Stock (at cost)
```

---

## Weighted Average Cost of Capital (WACC)

### Concept
Blended cost of debt and equity financing (corporate finance concept, **not GAAP**)

### Formula
```
WACC = (Weight of Debt × After-tax Cost of Debt) + (Weight of Equity × Cost of Equity)
```

### After-Tax Cost of Debt
```
After-tax Cost = Interest Rate × (1 - Tax Rate)
```

### Note
WACC is a valuation/finance concept, **not a financial statement measurement**

---

## Restrictions on Retained Earnings

### Reasons for Restrictions
- Debt covenants (loan agreements)
- State law requirements
- Treasury stock purchases (some states)
- Contractual restrictions

### Disclosure
- Disclosed in notes to financial statements
- Does **not reduce** reported RE balance
- Restricts amount available for dividends

### Appropriation (Optional)
May formally appropriate RE by creating separate account:
- Retained Earnings - Appropriated for Debt Covenant
- Retained Earnings - Unappropriated

## CPA Exam Tips

1. **Simple capital structure**: Only common stock → Report **basic EPS only**

2. **Complex capital structure**: Has potentially dilutive securities → Report **basic AND diluted EPS**

3. **Equity presentation order**: Preferred Stock, Common Stock, APIC, Retained Earnings, AOCI, (Treasury Stock)

4. **Treasury stock**: Contra-equity, reduces **outstanding** shares (not issued shares)

5. **Book value per share**: (Total equity - Preferred equity) / Common shares outstanding

6. **Restricted RE**: Disclosed in notes, **doesn't reduce** reported balance

---

## Common CPA Exam Scenarios

### Scenario 1: Classification
**Question**: Company has common stock, convertible bonds, and stock options. Simple or complex?
**Answer**: Complex (has potentially dilutive securities)

### Scenario 2: Book Value
**Question**: Total equity $500K, Preferred $50K, Common shares 25K. Book value per share?
**Answer**: ($500K - $50K) / 25K = $18 per share

### Scenario 3: Shares Outstanding
**Question**: Issued 10,000 shares, purchased 500 treasury shares. Outstanding?
**Answer**: 9,500 shares (issued - treasury)

## Summary

### Key Takeaways

1. **Simple capital structure**: Only common stock → Report basic EPS only

2. **Complex capital structure**: Has dilutive securities → Report basic and diluted EPS

3. **Equity presentation**: Preferred Stock, Common Stock, APIC, Retained Earnings, AOCI, less Treasury Stock

4. **Treasury stock**: Contra-equity, reduces outstanding (not issued) shares

5. **Book value per share**: (Total equity - Preferred equity) / Common shares outstanding

6. **Restricted RE**: Disclosed in notes, doesn't reduce reported balance

### Quick Reference

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Authorized shares | Maximum shares company can issue |
| Issued shares | Shares actually issued to shareholders |
| Outstanding shares | Issued shares minus treasury shares |
| Treasury shares | Company's own shares reacquired |
| Legal capital | Par value of issued shares |
