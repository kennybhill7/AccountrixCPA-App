# Going Concern Assessment - ASC 205-40

## Overview
Going concern assessment evaluates whether an entity can continue operations for a reasonable period of time (one year from financial statement issuance date).

## Management Responsibility
- **Who**: Management must evaluate going concern
- **When**: In connection with preparing financial statements each reporting period
- **Period**: At least 12 months from financial statement **issuance date** (not balance sheet date)

## Substantial Doubt Definition
Substantial doubt exists when conditions and events indicate it is **probable (>75% likelihood)** that the entity will be unable to meet its obligations as they become due within one year.

## Indicators of Going Concern Issues

### Negative Financial Trends
- Recurring operating losses
- Working capital deficiency
- Negative cash flows from operations
- Adverse key financial ratios

### Financial Difficulties
- Loan defaults or covenant violations
- Dividend arrearages
- Debt restructuring requirements
- Inability to obtain financing

### Internal Matters
- Work stoppages or labor disputes
- Substantial dependence on single project
- Loss of key personnel
- Uneconomic long-term commitments

### External Matters
- Legal proceedings or regulatory actions
- Loss of key customer or supplier
- Natural disasters affecting operations
- New laws or regulations

## Two-Step Assessment Process

### Step 1: Initial Assessment
**Question**: Are there conditions/events that raise substantial doubt?

- **If NO**: No disclosure required. Stop.
- **If YES**: Proceed to Step 2

### Step 2: Management Plans
**Question**: Will management's plans alleviate substantial doubt?

**Consideration**: Only consider plans that are:
- Within management's control
- Probable of being implemented effectively

**If YES (alleviated)**:
Disclose:
1. Conditions/events that raised substantial doubt
2. Management's evaluation of significance
3. Management's plans that alleviated the doubt

**Do NOT state** that substantial doubt exists (it was alleviated).

**If NO (not alleviated)**:
Disclose:
1. Principal conditions and events
2. Statement that conditions raise substantial doubt about going concern
3. Management's plans to address the issues

## Management Plans That May Alleviate Doubt

### Acceptable Plans
1. **Plans to dispose of assets**: Sell non-core assets for cash
2. **Plans to borrow money or restructure debt**: Obtain new financing, refinance existing debt
3. **Plans to reduce/delay expenditures**: Cut costs, delay capital projects
4. **Plans to increase ownership equity**: Issue new shares, obtain equity investments

### Requirements for Plans
- Must be **probable** of being implemented effectively
- Must be **within management's control**
- Cannot assume best-case outcomes or external events outside control

## Disclosure Requirements

### If Substantial Doubt is Alleviated
**Disclose**:
- Principal conditions and events that initially raised substantial doubt
- Management's evaluation of the significance of those conditions
- Management's plans that alleviated the substantial doubt

**Do NOT state**: That substantial doubt exists (because it was alleviated)

**Example Wording**:
"The Company has experienced recurring losses and has a working capital deficiency. However, management has executed a plan to sell non-core assets and restructure debt, which are probable of being completed. These actions alleviate substantial doubt about the Company's ability to continue as a going concern for at least one year from the issuance of the financial statements."

### If Substantial Doubt is NOT Alleviated
**Disclose**:
- Principal conditions and events raising substantial doubt
- Statement that conditions raise substantial doubt about the entity's ability to continue as a going concern
- Management's plans to address the conditions

**Example Wording**:
"The Company has experienced recurring losses from operations and has negative working capital. These conditions raise substantial doubt about the Company's ability to continue as a going concern for one year from the issuance of the financial statements. Management's plans to address these matters include obtaining additional financing and reducing operating expenses. However, there can be no assurance these plans will be successfully implemented."

## Liquidation Basis of Accounting

### When Used
- Liquidation is **imminent** (not just substantial doubt)
- Different from going concern assessment

### Measurement
- Assets at net realizable value
- Liabilities at settlement amounts
- Separate accounting framework (ASC 205-30)

## Audit Implications

### Auditor Responsibility
- Evaluate management's assessment
- May include explanatory paragraph in audit report

### If Substantial Doubt NOT Alleviated
- Auditor adds **emphasis-of-matter paragraph**
- This does **NOT modify** the opinion
- Opinion can still be unmodified if no other issues exist

## Comparison to IFRS

| US GAAP | IFRS |
|---------|------|
| Management assesses going concern | Management assesses going concern |
| Substantial doubt disclosure if not alleviated | Material uncertainties disclosed |
| 12 months from issuance date | 12 months from balance sheet date |
| Similar concept, slightly different terminology | Similar concept, slightly different terminology |

## CPA Exam Tips

1. **Evaluation period**: 12 months from financial statement **ISSUANCE** (not balance sheet date)
2. **Substantial doubt**: Probable (>75%) that entity cannot meet obligations
3. **Two-step process**:
   - Step 1: Is there substantial doubt?
   - Step 2: Do management's plans alleviate it?
4. **If alleviated**: Disclose conditions and plans, but **don't state** substantial doubt exists
5. **If not alleviated**: Disclose conditions, **state** substantial doubt exists, describe plans
6. **Management plans must be**: Within control and probable of implementation

## Key Takeaways

- Going concern assesses ability to continue operations for 12 months from financial statement issuance
- Substantial doubt = probable (>75%) entity cannot meet obligations within one year
- Two-step assessment: (1) Conditions raise doubt? (2) Management's plans alleviate?
- If alleviated: Disclose conditions and plans (don't state doubt exists)
- If not alleviated: Disclose conditions, state substantial doubt exists, describe plans
- Management plans must be probable and within management's control
