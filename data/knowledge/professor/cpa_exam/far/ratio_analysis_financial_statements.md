# Financial Statement Ratio Analysis

## Overview
Financial ratio analysis evaluates a company's financial performance and position using relationships between financial statement items.

## Categories of Ratios
1. Liquidity Ratios
2. Activity (Efficiency) Ratios
3. Profitability Ratios
4. Leverage (Solvency) Ratios
5. Market Ratios

---

## Liquidity Ratios
Measure ability to meet short-term obligations.

### Current Ratio
**Formula**: Current Assets ÷ Current Liabilities

**Interpretation**: Ability to pay short-term obligations. Higher is better.

**Benchmark**: > 1.0

**Example**: CA $200K, CL $100K → 2.0

### Quick Ratio (Acid-Test Ratio)
**Formula**: (Cash + Marketable Securities + Receivables) ÷ Current Liabilities

**Alternative**: (Current Assets - Inventory - Prepaid) ÷ Current Liabilities

**Interpretation**: Ability to pay with most liquid assets. Higher is better.

**Benchmark**: > 1.0

**Example**: Cash $50K, Receivables $70K, CL $100K → 1.2

### Working Capital
**Formula**: Current Assets - Current Liabilities

**Interpretation**: Dollar amount of net current assets. Positive is better.

**Example**: CA $200K, CL $100K → $100K

**Note**: This is a dollar amount, not a ratio.

## Activity (Efficiency) Ratios
Measure how efficiently assets are used.

### Receivables Turnover
**Formula**: Net Credit Sales ÷ Average Accounts Receivable

**Interpretation**: How many times receivables are collected during the period. Higher is better (faster collection).

**Example**: Sales $1M, Avg AR $100K → 10 times

### Days Sales in Receivables (DSO)
**Formula**: 365 ÷ Receivables Turnover

**Alternative**: (Average AR ÷ Net Credit Sales) × 365

**Interpretation**: Average days to collect receivables. Lower is better.

**Example**: Turnover 10 → 365 ÷ 10 = 36.5 days

### Inventory Turnover
**Formula**: Cost of Goods Sold ÷ Average Inventory

**Interpretation**: How many times inventory is sold during the period. Higher is better (less obsolescence risk).

**Example**: COGS $600K, Avg Inv $100K → 6 times

### Days Inventory on Hand (DIO)
**Formula**: 365 ÷ Inventory Turnover

**Interpretation**: Average days inventory is held. Lower is better.

**Example**: Turnover 6 → 365 ÷ 6 = 60.8 days

### Asset Turnover
**Formula**: Net Sales ÷ Average Total Assets

**Interpretation**: Efficiency of asset use to generate sales. Higher is better.

**Example**: Sales $1M, Avg Assets $500K → 2.0

## Profitability Ratios
Measure ability to generate profits.

### Gross Profit Margin
**Formula**: (Sales - COGS) ÷ Sales

**Alternative**: Gross Profit ÷ Sales

**Interpretation**: Profit before operating expenses. Higher is better.

**Example**: Sales $1M, COGS $600K → ($1M - $600K) ÷ $1M = 40%

### Operating Profit Margin
**Formula**: Operating Income ÷ Sales

**Interpretation**: Profit from core operations. Higher is better.

**Example**: Operating Income $200K, Sales $1M → 20%

### Net Profit Margin
**Formula**: Net Income ÷ Sales

**Interpretation**: Bottom-line profitability. Higher is better.

**Example**: NI $100K, Sales $1M → 10%

### Return on Assets (ROA)
**Formula**: Net Income ÷ Average Total Assets

**Interpretation**: Profit generated per dollar of assets. Higher is better.

**Example**: NI $100K, Avg Assets $500K → 20%

### Return on Equity (ROE)
**Formula**: Net Income ÷ Average Stockholders' Equity

**Interpretation**: Return to shareholders. Higher is better.

**Example**: NI $100K, Avg Equity $400K → 25%

**Note**: ROE is usually higher than ROA due to leverage.

---

## Leverage (Solvency) Ratios
Measure financial risk and ability to meet long-term obligations.

### Debt-to-Equity Ratio
**Formula**: Total Liabilities ÷ Total Stockholders' Equity

**Interpretation**: Financial leverage. Lower is less risky.

**Example**: Liabilities $300K, Equity $200K → 1.5

### Debt-to-Assets Ratio
**Formula**: Total Liabilities ÷ Total Assets

**Interpretation**: Portion of assets financed by debt. Lower is less risky.

**Example**: Liabilities $300K, Assets $500K → 0.6 or 60%

### Equity Multiplier
**Formula**: Total Assets ÷ Total Stockholders' Equity

**Interpretation**: Leverage measure (higher = more debt). Used in DuPont analysis.

**Example**: Assets $500K, Equity $200K → 2.5

### Times Interest Earned (Interest Coverage)
**Formula**: Operating Income (or EBIT) ÷ Interest Expense

**Interpretation**: Ability to cover interest payments. Higher is better.

**Benchmark**: > 2.0

**Example**: EBIT $200K, Interest $50K → 4.0

---

## Market Ratios
Measure market perceptions and shareholder returns.

### Price-to-Earnings (P/E) Ratio
**Formula**: Market Price per Share ÷ Earnings per Share

**Interpretation**: Valuation multiple. Higher may indicate growth expectations.

**Example**: Price $50, EPS $5 → P/E 10

### Dividend Yield
**Formula**: Annual Dividends per Share ÷ Market Price per Share

**Interpretation**: Cash return to investors. Higher may attract income investors.

**Example**: Dividend $2, Price $50 → 4%

### Payout Ratio
**Formula**: Dividends per Share ÷ Earnings per Share

**Alternative**: Total Dividends ÷ Net Income

**Interpretation**: Portion of earnings paid as dividends. Higher leaves less for reinvestment.

**Example**: Dividend $2, EPS $5 → 40%

---

## DuPont Analysis
Decomposes ROE into three components: profitability, efficiency, and leverage.

### Formula
**ROE = Net Profit Margin × Asset Turnover × Equity Multiplier**

**Calculation**:
ROE = (NI ÷ Sales) × (Sales ÷ Assets) × (Assets ÷ Equity)

### Interpretation
- **Net Profit Margin**: Profitability (how much profit per dollar of sales)
- **Asset Turnover**: Efficiency (how efficiently assets generate sales)
- **Equity Multiplier**: Leverage (how much assets are financed by equity vs. debt)

### Example
10% margin × 2.0 turnover × 2.5 multiplier = 50% ROE

### Benefits
- Identifies which component drives ROE
- Helps diagnose strengths and weaknesses
- Compares performance across companies

## CPA Exam Tips

1. **Current ratio** = CA ÷ CL. **Quick ratio** excludes inventory and prepaid.

2. **Turnover ratios**: Higher is better (faster activity). **Days ratios**: Lower is better.

3. **ROA** = NI ÷ Assets. **ROE** = NI ÷ Equity. ROE usually higher than ROA due to leverage.

4. **Times interest earned** = EBIT ÷ Interest (measures ability to cover interest).

5. **Gross margin** = (Sales - COGS) ÷ Sales. **Net margin** = NI ÷ Sales.

6. **DuPont formula**: ROE = Margin × Turnover × Multiplier

7. **Average balances**: Use average (beginning + ending) ÷ 2 for balance sheet items when calculating turnover ratios.

8. **Net credit sales**: Use net credit sales for receivables turnover (if given); may use total net sales if credit sales not specified.

---

## Key Takeaways

- **Liquidity**: Current ratio (CA/CL), Quick ratio ((CA-Inv-Prepaid)/CL)
- **Activity**: Receivables turnover (Sales/AR), Inventory turnover (COGS/Inv)
- **Profitability**: Gross margin ((Sales-COGS)/Sales), ROA (NI/Assets), ROE (NI/Equity)
- **Leverage**: Debt-to-equity (Liabilities/Equity), Times interest earned (EBIT/Interest)
- **Market**: P/E ratio (Price/EPS), Dividend yield (Dividend/Price)
- **DuPont**: ROE = Net margin × Asset turnover × Equity multiplier
