# Subsequent Events - ASC 855

## Definition

Events or transactions that occur **after the balance sheet date** but **before financial statements are issued or available to be issued**

## Evaluation Period

### Public Companies
Through the date financial statements are **issued** (filed with SEC)

### Private Companies
Through the date financial statements are **available to be issued** (given to users)

## Two Types of Subsequent Events

### Type I: Recognized Events

**Description**: Events that provide **additional evidence** about conditions that **existed AT the balance sheet date**

**Accounting**: **RECOGNIZE** in financial statements (adjust amounts)

**Rationale**: The condition existed at the balance sheet date; the subsequent event merely provides new information or evidence about that pre-existing condition

### Type II: Nonrecognized Events

**Description**: Events that provide evidence about conditions that **DID NOT exist** at the balance sheet date

**Accounting**: **DO NOT recognize** - **DISCLOSE only** if material

**Disclosure**: Nature of event and estimate of financial effect (or statement that estimate cannot be made)

## Decision Framework

### Key Question
**"Did the condition exist at the balance sheet date?"**

- **If YES** → Type I - Recognize (adjust financial statements)
- **If NO** → Type II - Disclose only (if material)

## Type I Examples (Recognize)

### 1. Settlement of Litigation

**Scenario**: Lawsuit pending at 12/31, settled on 1/15 for $500K. Financial statements issued 2/1.

**Analysis**:
- Condition at year-end? **Yes** - lawsuit existed
- Treatment: **Type I - Recognize**

**Accounting**: Adjust contingent liability to $500K

**Reasoning**: The lawsuit and underlying facts existed at 12/31; the settlement provides evidence of the liability amount

### 2. Customer Bankruptcy

**Scenario**: Receivable from customer at 12/31. Customer declares bankruptcy on 1/5.

**Analysis**:
- Condition at year-end? **Yes** - financial difficulties likely existed at 12/31
- Treatment: **Type I - Recognize**

**Accounting**: Increase allowance for doubtful accounts

**Reasoning**: Customer's financial distress existed at year-end, even if bankruptcy filing occurred after

### 3. Sale of Inventory Below Cost

**Scenario**: Inventory on hand at 12/31 valued at cost. In January, forced to sell at below cost due to obsolescence.

**Analysis**:
- Condition at year-end? **Yes** - inventory impairment existed at 12/31
- Treatment: **Type I - Recognize**

**Accounting**: Write down inventory to NRV at year-end

**Reasoning**: Sale price provides evidence that inventory was impaired at balance sheet date

### 4. Purchase Price Allocation

**Scenario**: Acquired business on 12/30. Finalized purchase price allocation in February.

**Analysis**:
- Condition at year-end? **Yes** - acquisition occurred at 12/31
- Treatment: **Type I - Recognize**

**Accounting**: Adjust preliminary allocation based on final determination

**Reasoning**: This is within the measurement period for business combination accounting

## Type II Examples (Disclose Only)

### 1. Fire Destroys Inventory

**Scenario**: Inventory on hand and intact at 12/31. Fire destroys it on 1/10.

**Analysis**:
- Condition at year-end? **No** - inventory was intact at 12/31
- Treatment: **Type II - Disclose only**

**Accounting**: Do NOT write down inventory in prior year statements. Disclose fire loss.

**Reasoning**: The damage did not exist at balance sheet date

### 2. Stock Issuance

**Scenario**: Company issues $10M of stock on 1/20.

**Analysis**:
- Condition at year-end? **No** - stock not issued until January
- Treatment: **Type II - Disclose only**

**Accounting**: Disclose issuance and use of proceeds

**Reasoning**: Transaction occurred after balance sheet date

### 3. Acquisition of Business

**Scenario**: Company acquires another business on 1/15.

**Analysis**:
- Condition at year-end? **No** - acquisition occurred after year-end
- Treatment: **Type II - Disclose only**

**Accounting**: Disclose nature and expected impact of acquisition

**Reasoning**: Transaction occurred after balance sheet date, not a pre-existing condition

### 4. Loss on Receivables from Sales After Year-End

**Scenario**: Company makes sales in January that result in bad debts.

**Analysis**:
- Condition at year-end? **No** - sales occurred after year-end
- Treatment: **Type II - Disclose only** (if material)

**Accounting**: Do not adjust prior year allowance

**Reasoning**: These are new transactions after the balance sheet date

## Type II Disclosure Requirements

When Type II events are material, disclose:

1. **Nature of the event**: Description of what occurred
2. **Financial effect**: Estimate of financial impact, or statement that such estimate cannot be made
3. **Pro forma information**: If appropriate (e.g., for major acquisitions)

### Example Disclosure

> "On January 15, 2024, the Company completed the acquisition of XYZ Corp for approximately $50 million in cash. XYZ Corp had revenues of $20 million and net income of $3 million for the year ended December 31, 2023. The acquisition is expected to be accretive to earnings in fiscal 2024."

## Comparison Table

| Feature | Type I (Recognized) | Type II (Nonrecognized) |
|---------|-------------------|------------------------|
| **Condition existed at BS date?** | Yes | No |
| **Accounting treatment** | Adjust financial statements | Disclose only (if material) |
| **Impact on amounts** | Changes BS/IS amounts | No change to BS/IS |
| **Examples** | Lawsuit settlement, customer bankruptcy, inventory NRV evidence | Stock issuance, fire, acquisition, new contracts |
| **Disclosure** | Normal financial statement presentation | Footnote disclosure |

## Journal Entry Examples

### Type I - Customer Bankruptcy

**Facts**:
- Receivable at 12/31: $100,000
- Customer bankruptcy declared 1/5
- Estimate 80% uncollectible

**Entry** (adjusting 12/31 financial statements):
```
DR Bad Debt Expense               $80,000
   CR Allowance for Doubtful Accounts     $80,000
```

**Effect**: Increases bad debt expense in prior year income statement

### Type I - Lawsuit Settlement

**Facts**:
- Lawsuit pending at 12/31, estimated liability $300K - $700K
- Settled 1/15 for $500K

**Entry** (adjusting 12/31 financial statements):
```
DR Loss on Litigation             $500,000
   CR Litigation Liability                 $500,000
```

**Effect**: Records specific liability amount instead of range

### Type II - Fire Loss (No Adjustment)

**Facts**:
- Inventory at 12/31: $200,000 (intact)
- Fire destroys inventory on 1/10

**Entry**: **None** for 12/31 financial statements

**Disclosure Only**:
> "On January 10, 2024, a fire destroyed inventory with a carrying value of approximately $200,000. The Company maintains insurance coverage and expects to recover a substantial portion of the loss."

## CPA Exam Tips

1. **Decision rule**: Ask "Did condition exist at balance sheet date?"
   - Yes = Type I (recognize)
   - No = Type II (disclose)

2. **Common Type I events**:
   - Lawsuit settlement (if lawsuit existed at year-end)
   - Customer bankruptcy (financial distress existed)
   - Inventory NRV evidence (impairment existed)
   - Purchase price allocation adjustments

3. **Common Type II events**:
   - Stock/debt issuance
   - Acquisitions after year-end
   - Casualties (fire, flood)
   - New contracts or agreements

4. **Evaluation period**:
   - Public: Through issuance date (SEC filing)
   - Private: Through available-to-be-issued date

5. **Both types must be evaluated**: Don't assume no subsequent events without considering the evaluation period

6. **Materiality matters**: Type II events only disclosed if material

## Common Mistakes

1. **Confusing the two types**:
   - Focus on whether condition existed at BS date
   - Don't be misled by when discovered

2. **Assuming fire/casualty is always Type II**:
   - Usually Type II (damage after BS date)
   - But if building already damaged at BS date and fire occurs after, provides evidence of pre-existing condition (Type I)

3. **Not evaluating through proper date**:
   - Must consider events through issuance/available date
   - Not just through auditor's report date

4. **Forgetting disclosure for Type II**:
   - Material Type II events require disclosure
   - Not just recognition vs. non-recognition decision

5. **Treating all lawsuit settlements as Type I**:
   - Only Type I if lawsuit existed at BS date
   - Lawsuit filed after year-end = Type II

## Summary

### Key Points

- **Subsequent events**: Events after BS date but before FS issued/available
- **Type I**: Condition existed at BS date → **Recognize** (adjust FS)
- **Type II**: Condition arose after BS date → **Disclose only** (if material)
- **Decision rule**: "Did condition exist at balance sheet date?"

### Quick Reference

- **Public companies**: Through date FS **issued** (SEC filing)
- **Private companies**: Through date FS **available to be issued**
