# Fair Value Measurements - ASC 820

## Definition

**Fair value** = The **price** that would be received to **sell an asset** or paid to **transfer a liability** in an **orderly transaction** between **market participants** at the **measurement date**

**Key characteristics**:
- **Exit price** (not entry price)
- **Market participant** assumptions (not entity-specific)
- **Orderly transaction** (not forced liquidation)
- **Current market conditions** at measurement date

## Fair Value Hierarchy

ASC 820 establishes a **three-level hierarchy** based on the **observability** of inputs used in valuation:

### Level 1: Quoted Prices in Active Markets

**Description**: Quoted prices in active markets for **identical assets or liabilities**

**Reliability**: **Most reliable** (highest priority)

**Adjustment**: **No adjustment permitted** - must use quoted price as-is

**Examples**:
- Actively traded stocks on NYSE or NASDAQ
- Exchange-traded bonds with active market
- Exchange-traded funds (ETFs)
- Commodities with active markets (gold, oil)

**Key characteristics**:
- **Identical** asset or liability
- **Active** market (regular transactions)
- **Quoted** price observable

**Preference**: **Use Level 1 if available** - highest priority in hierarchy

### Level 2: Observable Inputs

**Description**: Observable inputs **other than Level 1** quoted prices

**Reliability**: **Moderately reliable**

**Types of Level 2 inputs**:
- **Quoted prices for similar** (not identical) assets or liabilities in active markets
- **Quoted prices for identical or similar** assets in **inactive markets**
- Inputs other than quoted prices that are **observable** (interest rates, yield curves)
- Inputs **derived from or corroborated** by observable market data

**Examples**:
- Quoted prices for **similar bonds** (not identical)
- Interest rate swap valued using observable **LIBOR rates**
- Real estate appraised using **comparable sales**
- Corporate bonds quoted in **inactive markets**
- Foreign currency forward contracts using observable exchange rates

**Techniques**: May require **adjustments** to observable inputs to reflect specific characteristics

### Level 3: Unobservable Inputs

**Description**: **Unobservable inputs** reflecting entity's own assumptions about what market participants would use

**Reliability**: **Least reliable** (lowest priority)

**When used**: When **observable inputs not available** (little or no market activity)

**Examples**:
- Valuation of **privately-held company** using discounted cash flows (DCF)
- **Complex derivatives** with no market data
- Internally developed valuation models with significant unobservable assumptions
- Assets with no comparable market transactions

**Disclosure**: **Enhanced disclosures required** for Level 3 measurements
- Valuation techniques and inputs used
- Quantitative information about unobservable inputs
- Reconciliation of beginning to ending balances
- Sensitivity analysis

**Note**: Even for Level 3, must still use market participant assumptions (not purely entity-specific)

## Fair Value Hierarchy Summary Table

| Level | Description | Reliability | Examples | Adjustments |
|-------|-------------|-------------|----------|-------------|
| **Level 1** | Quoted prices, identical assets, active markets | Most reliable | NYSE stocks, exchange-traded bonds | Not permitted |
| **Level 2** | Observable inputs for similar assets or in inactive markets | Moderate | Comparable bond prices, interest rate swaps, comparable real estate sales | May be needed |
| **Level 3** | Unobservable inputs, entity's assumptions | Least reliable | Private company valuation, complex derivatives | Significant judgment |

## Valuation Techniques

ASC 820 permits three valuation approaches:

### 1. Market Approach

**Description**: Uses prices and relevant information from **market transactions** for identical or comparable assets/liabilities

**Techniques**:
- Comparable sales method
- Market multiples (P/E ratios, EBITDA multiples)
- Matrix pricing

**Example**: Valuing real estate using recent sales of comparable properties in same area

**Best for**: Assets with active or somewhat active markets

### 2. Income Approach

**Description**: Converts **future amounts** (cash flows, earnings) to a **single present value**

**Techniques**:
- Discounted cash flow (DCF)
- Present value models
- Option pricing models (Black-Scholes)
- Multi-period excess earnings method

**Example**: Valuing business or intangible asset using DCF of projected cash flows

**Best for**: Income-producing assets, businesses, certain intangibles

### 3. Cost Approach

**Description**: Amount required to **replace** the service capacity of the asset (**replacement cost**)

**Techniques**:
- Current replacement cost
- Depreciated replacement cost

**Example**: Valuing specialized equipment using cost to replace with equivalent capacity

**Best for**: Assets with no market and difficult to value via income approach

**Note**: Entity may use **multiple techniques** and weigh results as appropriate

## Highest and Best Use

### Principle for Nonfinancial Assets

**Fair value of nonfinancial asset** measured based on **highest and best use** by market participants

**Highest and best use** = Use that would maximize value of asset

**May differ from entity's current use**

### Example

**Current use**: Land used as parking lot generating $50,000/year

**Highest and best use**: Commercial development generating $500,000/year

**Valuation**: Use value based on **commercial development** (highest and best use), not parking lot use

**Reason**: Market participants would pay based on maximum value they could realize

### Not Applicable to Financial Assets/Liabilities

Financial assets and liabilities measured at price in **most advantageous market** (market that maximizes amount for asset or minimizes amount for liability)

## Principal (or Most Advantageous) Market

**Principal market**: Market with **greatest volume and level of activity** for asset or liability

**Most advantageous market**: Market that **maximizes** amount received for asset or **minimizes** amount paid for liability (after considering transaction costs)

**Presumption**: Entity's current market is principal market unless evidence otherwise

**Transaction costs**: Considered in determining most advantageous market, but **NOT included** in fair value measurement

**Transport costs**: **Included** in fair value if location characteristic of asset

## Disclosure Requirements

### All Fair Value Measurements

- **Fair value measurements by hierarchy level** (Level 1, 2, 3)
- **Valuation techniques and inputs** used
- Level in hierarchy for each measurement

### Level 3 Specific Disclosures

- **Reconciliation** of beginning to ending Level 3 balances
- Total gains or losses for period (realized and unrealized)
- Purchases, sales, issues, and settlements
- **Transfers into or out of Level 3**
- **Quantitative information** about significant unobservable inputs
- **Sensitivity analysis** (if change in input could significantly affect measurement)

### Transfers Between Levels

- Transfers between levels must be disclosed
- Reason for transfer
- Timing of recognition of transfer

## CPA Exam Tips

### Hierarchy Identification

**Level 1**:
- Key words: "Quoted price," "identical asset," "active market," "NYSE," "exchange-traded"
- **Most reliable**
- **Always use if available**

**Level 2**:
- Key words: "Similar asset," "comparable sales," "inactive market," "observable rates," "interest rate swap"
- **Moderately reliable**
- Requires some adjustments

**Level 3**:
- Key words: "Private company," "no market," "discounted cash flow," "management's assumptions," "unobservable"
- **Least reliable**
- Last resort when no observable data

### Key Concepts

**Fair value = Exit price** (what you'd receive to sell)

**Use Level 1 if available** (hierarchy prioritizes observable inputs)

**Highest and best use** for nonfinancial assets (may differ from current use)

**Market participant assumptions** (not entity-specific)

### Common Questions

**Q**: Company holds shares of stock actively traded on NYSE. What level?
**A**: Level 1 (quoted prices, identical, active market)

**Q**: Company values bond using quoted prices for similar bonds. What level?
**A**: Level 2 (similar, not identical)

**Q**: Company values private company investment using DCF with unobservable inputs. What level?
**A**: Level 3 (unobservable inputs)

**Q**: Land currently used for parking but highest and best use is commercial. How value?
**A**: Based on commercial use (highest and best use)

## Summary

### Key Points

- **Fair value** = Exit price in orderly transaction between market participants
- **Three-level hierarchy**:
  - **Level 1**: Quoted prices, identical, active markets (most reliable)
  - **Level 2**: Observable inputs for similar assets (moderate reliability)
  - **Level 3**: Unobservable inputs, entity's assumptions (least reliable)
- **Prefer Level 1** if available (highest priority)
- **Valuation techniques**: Market approach, income approach, cost approach
- **Highest and best use** for nonfinancial assets
- **Enhanced disclosures** for Level 3 measurements

**Hierarchy** = **"Q-O-U"**:
- Level 1: **Q**uoted (identical, active)
- Level 2: **O**bservable (similar, inactive)
