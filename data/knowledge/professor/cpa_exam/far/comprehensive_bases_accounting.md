# Comprehensive Bases of Accounting (OCBOA)

## Overview
OCBOA (Other Comprehensive Bases of Accounting) refers to comprehensive accounting frameworks other than GAAP that may be used for financial statement preparation.

**Definition**: Comprehensive basis of accounting other than GAAP

**When Used**:
- Private companies
- Special-purpose financial statements
- Contractual requirements
- Regulatory filings

---

## Types of OCBOA

### 1. Cash Basis

**Recognition Rules**:
- Revenue: When cash **received**
- Expenses: When cash **paid**

**Characteristics**:
- No accruals
- No receivables or payables recorded
- Simple to apply

**Example**:
```
Cash receipts: $1,000,000
Cash disbursements: $700,000
─────────────────────────
Income (Cash Basis): $300,000
```

**When Used**:
- Small businesses
- Professional practices (doctors, lawyers)
- When simplicity is valued

### 2. Modified Cash Basis

**Definition**: Primarily cash basis with selected accruals

**Hybrid Approach**:
- Cash basis for most transactions
- Accrual basis for selected items

**Example Modifications**:
- Cash basis + depreciation of fixed assets
- Cash basis + accrual of long-term liabilities
- Cash basis + inventory

**Flexibility**: Can customize (but must be comprehensive and consistently applied)

### 3. Tax Basis

**Recognition Rules**: Follow income tax regulations (IRS rules)

**Differences from GAAP**:
- Depreciation methods (MACRS vs. GAAP)
- Inventory methods
- Timing differences
- Revenue recognition

**When Used**:
- Partnership financial statements
- S corporations
- Compliance with tax requirements
- Pass-through entities

**Example**: Use tax depreciation (MACRS), tax inventory methods

### 4. Regulatory Basis

**Recognition Rules**: Follow regulatory agency requirements

**Examples**:
- **Insurance companies**: Statutory accounting (prescribed by state insurance regulators)
- **Banks**: Regulatory reporting (Federal Reserve, OCC requirements)
- **Utilities**: Rate-making accounting

**When Used**: Compliance with regulator requirements

### 5. Contractual Basis

**Recognition Rules**: Follow specific contract requirements

**Example**: Loan agreement requires cash-basis financial statements

**Limited Use**: Specific to contract parties

---

## Cash vs. Accrual Comparison

| Item | Cash Basis | Accrual Basis (GAAP) |
|------|-----------|---------------------|
| **Revenue recognition** | When cash received | When earned (performance obligation satisfied) |
| **Expense recognition** | When cash paid | When incurred (matched to revenue or consumed) |
| **Receivables/Payables** | Not recognized | Recognized |
| **Depreciation** | Usually not recorded (unless modified cash) | Recorded over useful life |
| **Prepaid expenses** | Not recorded | Recorded and amortized |
| **Accrued liabilities** | Not recorded | Recorded when incurred |

## Financial Statement Titles

### Important Rule
**Cannot use GAAP titles** if not prepared under GAAP

### GAAP Titles (Cannot Use for OCBOA)
- Balance Sheet
- Income Statement
- Statement of Cash Flows
- Statement of Stockholders' Equity

### Appropriate Cash Basis Titles
- **Statement of Assets and Liabilities Arising from Cash Transactions**
- **Statement of Revenue Collected and Expenses Paid**
- **Statement of Cash Receipts and Disbursements**

### Appropriate Tax Basis Titles
- **Statement of Assets, Liabilities, and Equity - Income Tax Basis**
- **Statement of Revenue and Expenses - Income Tax Basis**

### Principle
Titles must **clearly indicate** the basis of accounting used

## Disclosure Requirements

### 1. Basis Description
Disclose the basis of accounting used and how it differs from GAAP

**Example**:
"These financial statements have been prepared on the cash basis of accounting, which is a comprehensive basis of accounting other than generally accepted accounting principles. Under the cash basis, revenues are recognized when received rather than when earned, and expenses are recognized when paid rather than when incurred."

### 2. Material Differences from GAAP
Describe material differences from GAAP (qualitatively or quantitatively)

**Example**:
"The most significant differences from GAAP are: (1) accounts receivable and accounts payable are not recorded, (2) fixed assets are expensed when purchased rather than capitalized and depreciated, and (3) long-term debt is not recorded until payments are made."

### 3. Consistency
Disclose if basis changed from prior period

## Audit Reporting on OCBOA Statements

### Special Purpose Framework Opinion
Auditor issues special purpose framework opinion (not GAAP opinion)

### Emphasis-of-Matter Paragraph
Audit report emphasizes financial statements prepared using special purpose framework

**Example Report Language**:
"As described in Note X, these financial statements were prepared in accordance with the cash basis of accounting, which is a basis of accounting other than accounting principles generally accepted in the United States of America."

### Distribution Restrictions
May restrict distribution if prepared for specific purpose

**Example Restriction**:
"This report is intended solely for the information and use of [specified parties] and is not intended to be and should not be used by anyone other than these specified parties."

## GAAP vs. OCBOA Decision

### GAAP
**Required for**:
- Public companies
- Companies seeking external investors
- IPO candidates

**Advantages**:
- Most comprehensive information
- Widely understood and comparable
- Required by lenders and investors in many cases

**Disadvantages**:
- Complex
- Costly to implement and maintain

### OCBOA
**Allowed for**:
- Private companies
- Limited distribution financial statements
- Specific contractual/regulatory purposes

**Advantages**:
- Simpler to apply
- Less costly
- May meet specific needs (tax, regulatory, contractual)

**Disadvantages**:
- Less comprehensive
- Not comparable across entities
- May not be accepted by all lenders/investors

### Entity Choice
Entity chooses basis (unless required by contract/regulator)

## CPA Exam Tips

1. **OCBOA** = Other Comprehensive Basis of Accounting (not GAAP)

2. **Types**: Cash, modified cash, tax, regulatory, contractual

3. **Cash basis**: Revenue when **received**, expenses when **paid**. No accruals.

4. **Tax basis**: Follow **IRS rules** (depreciation, inventory, etc.)

5. **Financial statement titles must differ from GAAP** (can't say "Balance Sheet" if cash basis)

6. **Disclose**: Basis used and differences from GAAP

---

## Common CPA Exam Scenarios

### Scenario 1: Recognition Timing
**Question**: Company uses cash basis. Invoice $10K sent in December, cash received January. When recognize revenue?
**Answer**: January (when cash received)

### Scenario 2: Title Appropriateness
**Question**: Cash basis statements titled "Income Statement" - appropriate?
**Answer**: No. Must use title that indicates cash basis (e.g., "Statement of Revenue Collected and Expenses Paid")

### Scenario 3: Audit Report
**Question**: Auditor audits cash basis statements. Can issue GAAP opinion?
**Answer**: No. Must issue special purpose framework opinion

## Summary

### Key Takeaways

1. **OCBOA**: Comprehensive basis other than GAAP (cash, modified cash, tax, regulatory, contractual)

2. **Cash basis**: Revenue when received, expenses when paid. No accruals.

3. **Modified cash**: Cash basis with selected accruals (flexible)

4. **Tax basis**: Follow IRS rules

5. **Financial statement titles must distinguish from GAAP** (can't use "Balance Sheet," "Income Statement" if not GAAP)

6. **Disclose**: Basis used and differences from GAAP

### Quick Reference

| Basis | Revenue Recognition | Expense Recognition | Fixed Assets |
|-------|-------------------|-------------------|-------------|
| **Cash** | When received | When paid | Not recorded |
| **Modified Cash** | When received | When paid | May depreciate |
| **Tax** | Per IRS rules | Per IRS rules | MACRS |
| **GAAP** | When earned | When incurred | Depreciated |
