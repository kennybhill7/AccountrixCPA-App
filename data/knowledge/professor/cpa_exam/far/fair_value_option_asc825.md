# Fair Value Option - ASC 825

## Overview
The fair value option (FVO) is an **irrevocable election** to measure eligible financial assets and liabilities at fair value, with changes in fair value reported in net income.

## Purpose
- Reduce volatility in financial statements by allowing consistent measurement of related assets/liabilities
- Simplify hedge accounting
- Provide flexibility in financial reporting

## Key Principle
**OPTIONAL** - not required. Once elected for an instrument, the election is **irrevocable**.

## Eligible Instruments

### Can Elect FVO For:
1. **Financial assets**: Receivables, investments in debt and equity securities
2. **Financial liabilities**: Notes payable, bonds payable
3. **Firm commitments** involving financial instruments
4. **Certain nonfinancial items**: Insurance contracts, warranties

### CANNOT Elect FVO For (Ineligible):
1. **Investment in subsidiary** that requires consolidation
2. **Obligations for pension/OPEB plans**
3. **Financial assets/liabilities under leases** (ASC 842)
4. **Demand deposit liabilities** (for banks)
5. **Equity method investments**

## Election Timing

### When to Elect
- At acquisition/issuance of the instrument
- Upon certain reconsideration events

### Instrument-by-Instrument
- Applied to individual instruments
- **Not all-or-nothing**: Can elect for some but not all similar instruments

### Irrevocable
- **Cannot revoke** once elected
- Permanent decision for that instrument

## Accounting Treatment

### Initial Measurement
Fair value (transaction price)

### Subsequent Measurement
Fair value at each reporting period

### Unrealized Gains/Losses
Reported in **NET INCOME** (not OCI)

### Interest and Dividends
- May report separately from fair value changes
- Or may report together with fair value changes

## Comparison to Other Methods

### Without FVO - Financial Assets
May be classified as:
- **Trading**: FV through net income
- **AFS (Available-for-Sale)**: FV through OCI
- **Held-to-Maturity**: Amortized cost

### Without FVO - Financial Liabilities
Typically measured at **amortized cost**

### With FVO
Always **fair value** with changes through **net income** (similar to trading securities)

## Examples

### Example 1: Financial Asset

**Facts**: Purchase AFS debt securities for $100,000. Elect FVO. Year-end FV is $110,000.

**Without FVO (AFS treatment)**:
```
DR Investment                    $10,000
   CR OCI                                   $10,000
(Unrealized gain to OCI)
```

**With FVO**:
```
DR Investment                    $10,000
   CR Unrealized Gain (NI)                  $10,000
(Unrealized gain to net income)
```

**Note**: FVO removes AFS classification and OCI treatment.

### Example 2: Financial Liability

**Facts**: Issue note payable for $100,000. Elect FVO. Year-end FV is $95,000 (company's credit risk increased).

**Entry**:
```
DR Note Payable                  $5,000
   CR Gain on Change in FV (NI)             $5,000
```

**Counterintuitive**: Company's creditworthiness worsens → liability FV decreases → recognize **gain** in net income

## Upfront Costs
Transaction costs and fees are **expensed immediately** (not added to carrying amount like under amortized cost method).

## Disclosure Requirements

1. Fair value and changes in fair value for items measured at FVO
2. Differences between fair value and contractual amounts
3. Reasons for electing FVO

## Hedge Accounting Alternative
FVO can be used instead of complex hedge accounting if a hedging relationship exists.

## Own Credit Risk for Liabilities

### The Issue
If a company's creditworthiness declines:
- Liability fair value decreases
- Company recognizes a **gain** (counterintuitive)

### ASC 825 Treatment
Gain/loss reported in **net income** (no separate treatment for own credit risk)

### Note on ASC 815
Different rules for derivatives - own credit adjustments may go to OCI under derivative accounting.

## Fair Value Measurement
Fair value determined using ASC 820 guidance:
- Level 1: Quoted prices in active markets
- Level 2: Observable inputs other than quoted prices
- Level 3: Unobservable inputs

## CPA Exam Tips

1. **FVO = OPTIONAL** election to measure at FV with changes in NI
2. **Eligible**: Financial assets/liabilities
3. **Ineligible**: Subsidiaries, leases, equity method investments, pension obligations
4. **Once elected, IRREVOCABLE**
5. **Changes in FV go to NET INCOME** (not OCI)
6. **Similar to trading securities** treatment (FV through NI) for assets
7. **Counterintuitive for liabilities**: Credit worsens → FV decreases → Gain in NI
8. **Transaction costs**: Expensed immediately, not capitalized

## Common CPA Exam Scenarios

### Scenario 1: When to Elect
**Question**: When can FVO be elected?
**Answer**: At acquisition/issuance or upon reconsideration events

### Scenario 2: Where Do Changes Go?
**Question**: Where are unrealized gains/losses reported under FVO?
**Answer**: Net income (not OCI)

### Scenario 3: Can You Change Your Mind?
**Question**: Can you revoke the FVO election?
**Answer**: No, it's irrevocable

### Scenario 4: Liability FV Decreases
**Question**: Company's credit rating drops, liability FV decreases by $10,000. What's the entry?
**Answer**: DR Note Payable $10,000, CR Gain $10,000 (recognize gain in NI)

## Key Takeaways

- Fair value option: **Optional, irrevocable** election to measure at FV with changes in NI
- **Eligible**: Financial assets/liabilities
- **Ineligible**: Subsidiaries, leases, equity method investments
- Changes in FV reported in **NET INCOME** (not OCI)
- Elected **instrument-by-instrument** at acquisition/issuance
- **Transaction costs expensed** immediately
- Similar to **trading securities** (FV through NI) for assets
- **Counterintuitive for liabilities**: Creditworthiness worsens → Gain recognized
