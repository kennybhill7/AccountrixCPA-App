# Intangible Assets - ASC 350

## Definition

Assets lacking physical substance but providing future economic benefits

**Recognition criteria**:
1. Meets definition of asset
2. Specifically identifiable (separable from entity)
3. Future benefits probable
4. Cost measurable

## Two Categories

### Finite-Lived Intangibles

**Definition**: Intangibles with determinable useful life

**Examples**:
- Patents
- Copyrights
- Franchises (with expiration)
- Customer lists
- Non-compete agreements

**Accounting**: **AMORTIZE** over useful life (not to exceed legal/contractual life)

**Amortization method**: Straight-line unless another method more appropriate

**Residual value**: Usually **zero** unless third party committed to purchase or active market exists

**Impairment test**: When indicators present (similar to long-lived assets - ASC 360)

### Indefinite-Lived Intangibles

**Definition**: Intangibles with no foreseeable limit on useful life

**Examples**:
- Trademarks (renewed indefinitely)
- Trade names
- Broadcasting licenses (renewable)
- Goodwill (separate guidance)

**Accounting**: **DO NOT amortize**. Test for impairment **ANNUALLY**.

**Impairment test**: One-step - Compare FV to carrying amount. If FV < CA, recognize impairment loss.

## Internally Developed vs. Purchased

### Purchased Intangibles

**Capitalize** at cost (purchase price + directly attributable costs)

### Internally Developed

**General rule**: **EXPENSE as incurred** (R&D costs, startup costs, advertising)

**Exceptions**:
- **Software**: Capitalize costs after technological feasibility established (ASC 985)
- **Website**: Capitalize certain development costs in development stage

## Specific Intangibles

### Patents

**Legal life**: 20 years from filing date

**Accounting**: Amortize over **SHORTER of legal life or useful life**

**Internally developed**: Expense R&D costs. Capitalize only legal/registration fees.

**Purchased**: Capitalize purchase price, amortize over useful life

**Example**:
```
Purchase patent for $100,000, useful life 10 years
Annual amortization: $100,000 / 10 = $10,000

DR Amortization Expense                   $10,000
   CR Patent (or Accumulated Amortization)        $10,000
```

### Copyrights

**Legal life**: Life of author plus 70 years

**Accounting**: Amortize over expected period of benefit (usually much shorter than legal life)

### Trademarks

**Legal life**: 10 years, renewable indefinitely

**Accounting**:
- **If intend to renew indefinitely**: Indefinite-lived (no amortization)
- **If not renewing**: Finite-lived (amortize over expected use)

**Internally developed**: Expense advertising/promotion costs. Capitalize only legal/registration fees.

### Franchises

**Finite term**: Amortize over franchise period

**Indefinite term**: No amortization, test for impairment annually

### Customer Lists

**Purchased**: Capitalize and amortize over expected benefit period

**Internally developed**: Expense as incurred

## Research and Development (R&D)

### General Rule

**Expense ALL R&D costs as incurred**

**Definition**:
- **Research**: Planned investigation to discover new knowledge
- **Development**: Translation of research into design for new product/process

### Exceptions

**Materials/equipment with ALTERNATIVE future use**: Capitalize as asset, depreciate

**R&D performed for others under contract**: Capitalize as inventory or contract asset

**Software development exception**: Expense until technological feasibility. Capitalize thereafter (ASC 985).

## Startup Costs

**Definition**: One-time costs to begin operations (organizing corporation, training, pre-opening costs)

**Accounting**: **EXPENSE as incurred** (not capitalized as intangible asset)

## Amortization Calculation

**Formula**:
```
Annual Amortization = Cost / Useful Life
```

**Residual value**: Usually zero for intangibles

**Example**:
```
Patent: $100,000
Useful life: 10 years
Residual value: $0

Annual amortization: $100,000 / 10 = $10,000
```

## Impairment Testing

### Finite-Lived Intangibles

**When test**: When indicators of impairment present

**Test**: Two-step (similar to long-lived assets - ASC 360):
1. **Recoverability test**: Compare sum of undiscounted future cash flows to carrying amount
2. **If not recoverable**: Impairment loss = Carrying amount - Fair value

**No reversal**: Impairment losses cannot be reversed

### Indefinite-Lived Intangibles

**When test**: Test **ANNUALLY** (and when indicators present)

**Test**: One-step - FV vs. CA
- If FV < CA, impairment loss = CA - FV

**No reversal**: Impairment losses cannot be reversed

**Qualitative assessment**: May perform qualitative first. If more-likely-than-not FV > CA, skip quantitative test.

## Comparison to Goodwill

| Feature | Intangibles | Goodwill |
|---------|-------------|----------|
| **Identifiability** | Separately identifiable | Not separately identifiable |
| **Life** | May be finite or indefinite | Always indefinite |
| **Impairment test** | Asset level | Reporting unit level |

## Comprehensive Example

**Facts**: Company acquires:
- Patent: $50,000 (useful life 10 years)
- Trademark: $30,000 (indefinite - intend to renew)
- Customer list: $20,000 (5-year life)

**Year 1 Amortization**:
```
Patent: $50,000 / 10 = $5,000
Trademark: $0 (indefinite - no amortization)
Customer list: $20,000 / 5 = $4,000
Total: $9,000
```

**Year 1 Entry**:
```
DR Amortization Expense                    $9,000
   CR Accumulated Amortization - Patent            $5,000
   CR Accumulated Amortization - Customer List     4,000
```

**Impairment Testing**:
- Trademark: Tested **annually** for impairment
- Patent and customer list: Tested **when indicators present**

## CPA Exam Tips

### Key Decision Rules

- **Finite-lived**: AMORTIZE
- **Indefinite-lived**: NO amortization, annual impairment test
- **Internally developed** (except software/website): EXPENSE as incurred
- **R&D costs**: EXPENSE as incurred (except materials/equipment with alternative use)
- **Patents**: Capitalize purchase price or legal fees. Amortize over shorter of legal or useful life.
- **Trademarks**: If renewable indefinitely and intend to renew = indefinite (no amortization)
- **Startup costs**: Expense as incurred (not capitalized)

### Impairment Summary

- **Finite-lived** = two-step (like PPE)
- **Indefinite-lived** = one-step (FV vs CA)

### Common Mistakes

- Amortizing indefinite-lived intangibles (don't amortize)
- Capitalizing R&D costs (should expense)
- Capitalizing startup costs (should expense)
- Using legal life instead of useful life (use shorter)
- Confusing intangible impairment with goodwill impairment

## Summary

### Key Points

- **Finite-lived**: Amortize over useful life, test when indicators
- **Indefinite-lived**: No amortization, test annually
- **Internally developed**: Generally expense (except software, website)
- **R&D**: Expense as incurred
- **Patents**: Amortize over shorter of legal (20 years) or useful life
- **Trademarks**: Indefinite if renewable and intend to renew
- **Impairment**: Finite = two-step. Indefinite = one-step (FV vs CA).

### Quick Reference

| Intangible | Legal Life | Finite or Indefinite | Amortize? |
|------------|------------|---------------------|-----------|
| Patent | 20 years | Finite | Yes - over shorter of legal or useful |
| Copyright | Author + 70 years | Finite | Yes - over expected benefit |
| Trademark | 10 years, renewable | Depends on intent | If indefinite: No. If finite: Yes |
| Franchise | Varies | Depends on term | If term expires: Yes. If indefinite: No |
| Customer list | N/A | Finite | Yes - over expected benefit |
| R&D | N/A | Expense | Expense as incurred |
