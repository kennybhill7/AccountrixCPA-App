# Selection and Application of Accounting Policies

## Definition
Specific principles, bases, conventions, rules, and practices applied in preparing financial statements

## Key Principle
Select policies that result in information that is **relevant** and **faithfully represents** economic substance

## GAAP Hierarchy

### Category A (Authoritative)
**FASB Accounting Standards Codification**
- Primary source of authoritative GAAP

### Category B (Non-Authoritative)
If no specific guidance in Codification, consider:
- FASB Concepts Statements
- AICPA Issues Papers
- Other guidance

### Professional Judgment
If no authoritative guidance, use judgment based on conceptual framework

## Policy Selection Factors
- Industry practices
- Comparability with peers
- Consistency with entity's operations
- Relevance to users
- Faithful representation
- Neutrality (not biased)

## Common Policy Choices

| Area | Options |
|------|---------|
| **Inventory** | FIFO, weighted average |
| **Depreciation** | Straight-line, declining balance, units of production |
| **Revenue Recognition** | Point in time vs over time (based on ASC 606 criteria) |
| **Investments** | Fair value through NI, fair value through OCI, amortized cost |
| **Consolidation** | Equity method, consolidation, fair value option |

## Disclosure Requirements

### Note 1: Summary of Significant Accounting Policies
Typically the first note to financial statements

### Required Disclosures
- Basis of consolidation
- Depreciation methods
- Amortization of intangibles
- Inventory pricing
- Recognition of revenue
- Income taxes
- Use of estimates

### New Policies
Disclose newly adopted policies or changes

## Consistency Principle

**Require Consistency**: Apply same policies from period to period

**Changes Allowed**: Can change if new policy preferable (requires retrospective application typically)

**Disclosure**: Changes in policies require disclosure and restatement

## Substance Over Form

**Principle**: Select policies that reflect economic substance, not just legal form

**Example**: Lease accounted as finance lease if transfers substantially all risks/rewards, even if legal title doesn't transfer

## Conservatism in Policy Selection

**Historical**: When in doubt, recognize losses but not gains

**Current Framework**: Neutrality emphasized (don't deliberately understate or overstate)

**Practical**: Conservative policies may still be selected if justified

## Industry-Specific Policies

**Specialized Industries**: Construction, insurance, banking, oil & gas have specific guidance

**Follow Industry Practice**: Enhances comparability within industry

## Policy Application Errors

| Type | Treatment |
|------|-----------|
| **Error** | Mistake in applying policy → Correct retrospectively |
| **Change in Policy** | Voluntary change from one acceptable method to another → Retrospective |
| **Change in Estimate** | Change based on new information → Prospective |

## Management Judgment

### Areas Requiring Judgment
- Useful lives of assets
- Collectibility of receivables
- Fair value measurements
- Loss contingencies
- Revenue recognition over time

### Document Rationale
Document basis for judgments (internal control, audit trail)

## CPA Exam Tips

1. **Accounting policies**: Specific principles applied in preparing financial statements

2. **GAAP hierarchy**: ASC is authoritative. If no guidance, use judgment and concepts.

3. **Note 1**: Summary of significant accounting policies (required disclosure)

4. **Consistency**: Apply same policies unless change is preferable (retrospective)

5. **Substance over form**: Policies reflect economic substance

6. **Industry practices**: Consider comparability within industry

## Summary

### Key Points
- Accounting policies: Specific principles applied in preparing FS
- GAAP hierarchy: ASC authoritative, concepts and judgment if no guidance
- Note 1: Disclose significant accounting policies
- Consistency: Same policies period to period unless change preferable
- Substance over form: Reflect economic substance
- Selection factors: Relevance, faithful representation, comparability, neutrality
