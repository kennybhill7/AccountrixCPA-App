# Interim Financial Reporting - ASC 270

## Overview

**Interim periods**: Periods less than a full fiscal year (quarterly, monthly)

**Key principle**: Each interim period is an **integral part** of the annual period

## Integral View Approach

### Concept
Interim periods are **not standalone** periods - they are components of the annual period

### Implications
- Revenues and expenses should relate to the annual period
- May require estimates, accruals, deferrals to achieve proper annual results
- Annual operating expenses may be allocated across interim periods

### Contrast: Discrete View
*Not used under US GAAP* - Would treat each interim period independently

## Revenue Recognition

### General Rule
Recognize revenue in interim periods using **same basis** as annual

### Seasonal Revenue

**Disclosure Required**: If material seasonal variations

**Example**: Retail with heavy Q4 holiday sales should disclose seasonal nature

**No Special Accounting**: Record revenue when earned, even if concentrated in one quarter

## Cost of Goods Sold

### General Rule
Use same inventory costing method as annual (FIFO, LIFO, weighted-average)

### LIFO Liquidation - Interim Only

**Problem**: Interim LIFO liquidation expected to be replaced by year-end

**Solution**:
- Do NOT reflect liquidation profit in interim COGS
- Include cost of expected replacement (even at higher cost)

**Example**:
```
Q1: Sell 1,000 units from base LIFO layer at $10/unit
    Expect to replace by year-end at $15/unit

Interim COGS: 1,000 × $15 = $15,000 (not $10,000)
```

### Inventory Market Declines (LCM)

**Temporary decline**:
- Do NOT recognize loss if expected to reverse by year-end

**Other than temporary decline**:
- Recognize loss in interim period
- If recovery in later interim period, recognize gain (up to loss previously recognized)

**Example**:
```
Q1: Inventory cost $100K, market $80K (temporary)
    No loss recognized in Q1

Q1: Inventory cost $100K, market $80K (permanent)
    Recognize $20K loss in Q1

Q2: Market recovers to $90K
    Recognize $10K gain in Q2 (limited to prior loss)
```

## Income Tax Expense

### Annual Effective Tax Rate (AETR) Approach

**Concept**: Estimate annual effective tax rate, apply to year-to-date income

### Steps

1. **Estimate annual effective tax rate (AETR)**:
   ```
   AETR = Estimated Annual Tax / Estimated Annual Pre-Tax Income
   ```

2. **Calculate year-to-date tax expense**:
   ```
   YTD Tax Expense = YTD Pre-Tax Income × AETR
   ```

3. **Calculate current quarter expense**:
   ```
   Current Quarter = YTD Tax Expense - Prior Quarters' Tax Expense
   ```

### Example - Q1

**Facts**:
- Q1 pre-tax income: $100,000
- Estimated annual pre-tax income: $500,000
- Estimated annual tax: $125,000

**Calculation**:
```
AETR = $125,000 / $500,000 = 25%

Q1 Tax Expense = $100,000 × 25% = $25,000
```

### Example - Q2 (Year-to-Date)

**Facts**:
- Q1 pre-tax income: $100,000
- Q2 pre-tax income: $120,000
- YTD pre-tax income: $220,000
- Revised estimated annual pre-tax income: $480,000
- Revised estimated annual tax: $120,000

**Calculation**:
```
Revised AETR = $120,000 / $480,000 = 25%

YTD Tax Expense (through Q2) = $220,000 × 25% = $55,000

Q2 Tax Expense = $55,000 - $25,000 (Q1) = $30,000
```

### Ordinary Income vs. Unusual Items

**Ordinary income**: Use AETR approach

**Unusual or infrequent items**: Tax effect recognized in the interim period when the item occurs (discrete treatment)

## Annual Operating Expenses

### Expenses Allocated Across Interim Periods

Examples:
- Annual bonuses
- Year-end adjustments
- Certain insurance expenses
- Property taxes

### Method
Estimate annual amount, allocate proportionately to interim periods

### Example - Annual Bonus

**Facts**:
- Estimated annual bonus: $120,000
- Q1 revenue: $500,000
- Estimated annual revenue: $2,000,000

**Calculation**:
```
Q1 Bonus Expense = $120,000 × ($500,000 / $2,000,000)
                 = $120,000 × 25%
                 = $30,000
```

### Example - Property Tax

**Facts**:
- Property tax bill: $36,000 for July 1 - June 30 fiscal year
- Received and paid in Q3 (January)

**Treatment**:
```
Q1 (Jul-Sep): Accrue $9,000 ($36,000 / 4)
Q2 (Oct-Dec): Accrue $9,000
Q3 (Jan-Mar): Recognize $9,000 (bill received and paid)
Q4 (Apr-Jun): Recognize $9,000
```

## Inventory Costs

### Product Costs
Allocate based on production or sales activity

### Variances
Planned volume variances deferred if expected to be absorbed by year-end

### Example - Volume Variance

**Facts**:
- Q1: Unfavorable volume variance $50,000 due to low production
- Expect full capacity by year-end

**Treatment**:
- Defer variance in Q1 (expense over remaining quarters)
- If variance expected to exist at year-end, expense in quarter incurred

## Advertising Costs

### General Rule
Expense as incurred

### Exception
If clear benefits to subsequent interim periods within the year, may defer and allocate

**Practical**: Most advertising expensed when incurred in interim periods

## Contingencies

### Recognition
Same criteria as annual financial statements (ASC 450)

### Changes in Estimates
If contingency estimate changes, adjust in the interim period of the change

## Accounting Changes

### Change in Accounting Principle
- Retrospective application (like annual)
- Restate prior interim periods if presented

### Change in Estimate
- Prospective application
- Recognize in current and future periods

### Example - Depreciation Estimate Change

**Facts**:
- Change in useful life estimate occurs in Q2
- New depreciation: $40,000 annually

**Treatment**:
```
Q1: Previously reported depreciation (not restated)
Q2: Depreciation = $10,000 (1/4 of $40,000)
Q3: Depreciation = $10,000
Q4: Depreciation = $10,000
```

## Segment Reporting

### Required for Interim Reports
If entity required to report segment information annually, must report in interim periods

### Minimum Disclosures
- Revenue from external customers
- Intersegment revenues
- Segment profit or loss
- Total assets (if material change)
- Changes in segment structure

## Disclosure Requirements

### Minimum Disclosures in Condensed Interim Statements

1. Sales or gross revenues
2. Income tax expense
3. Extraordinary items and unusual/infrequent items
4. Net income
5. Comprehensive income
6. Basic and diluted EPS
7. Seasonal revenues, costs, expenses
8. Significant changes in estimates
9. Debt/equity issuances, repurchases
10. Segment information
11. Material changes in contingencies
12. Material changes in accounting policies

## CPA Exam Tips

1. **Integral view**: Interim periods are part of annual period
   - Allocate annual costs across quarters
   - Use AETR for tax expense

2. **AETR calculation**:
   - Calculate annual rate
   - Apply to YTD income
   - Subtract prior quarters to get current

3. **LIFO liquidation**:
   - If temporary (will replace), use replacement cost in interim COGS
   - If permanent, use actual liquidation cost

4. **Inventory market declines**:
   - Temporary: Defer loss
   - Permanent: Recognize loss
   - Recovery: Recognize gain up to prior loss

5. **Annual expenses**:
   - Bonuses, property taxes: Allocate across quarters
   - Use estimated annual amounts

6. **Seasonal revenue**:
   - Disclose, but don't smooth across quarters
   - Recognize when earned

7. **Common tested**:
   - AETR calculation (especially Q2 with revised estimates)
   - LIFO interim liquidation treatment
   - Annual expense allocation

## Common Mistakes

1. **Using discrete view**: Each interim period is not standalone

2. **Not updating AETR**: Must revise each quarter based on updated estimates

3. **YTD vs. Quarter calculation**:
   - Calculate YTD tax expense first
   - Then subtract prior quarters

4. **LIFO liquidation**: Forgetting to use replacement cost when liquidation is temporary

5. **Recognizing temporary inventory losses**: Should defer if expected to reverse

6. **Not allocating annual expenses**: Must estimate and allocate bonuses, taxes, etc.

## Summary

### Key Concepts

- **Integral View**: Interim periods are parts of annual period, not standalone
- **AETR**: Estimate annual tax rate, apply to YTD income, adjust quarterly
- **Annual Expenses**: Estimate and allocate across quarters
- **LIFO Liquidation**: Use replacement cost if temporary
- **Inventory Losses**: Defer temporary declines, recognize permanent

### Key Formulas

**Annual Effective Tax Rate**:
```
AETR = Estimated Annual Tax / Estimated Annual Pre-Tax Income
YTD Tax Expense = YTD Income × AETR
Current Quarter Tax = YTD Tax - Prior Quarters' Tax
```

**Annual Expense Allocation**:
```
Quarterly Expense = Annual Expense × (Quarterly Activity / Annual Activity)
```

### Quick Reference

| Item | Treatment |
|------|-----------|
| Revenue | Recognize when earned (same as annual) |
| Seasonal revenue | Disclose; don't smooth |
| Tax expense | Use AETR approach |
| Annual bonuses | Estimate and allocate |
| Property taxes | Accrue over periods benefited |
| LIFO liquidation (temporary) | Use replacement cost |
| Inventory loss (temporary) | Defer |
| Inventory loss (permanent) | Recognize; recovery allowed |
| Accounting change (principle) | Retrospective |
| Accounting change (estimate) | Prospective |
