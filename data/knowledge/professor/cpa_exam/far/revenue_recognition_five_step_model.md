# ASC 606 Five-Step Revenue Recognition Model

## Core Principle

**Recognize revenue to depict the transfer of promised goods or services to customers in an amount that reflects the consideration to which the entity expects to be entitled.**

## The Five Steps

### Overview

| Step | Title | Key Question |
|------|-------|--------------|
| **1** | Identify the contract with a customer | Is there an enforceable agreement? |
| **2** | Identify performance obligations | What distinct goods/services are promised? |
| **3** | Determine the transaction price | How much consideration is expected? |
| **4** | Allocate the transaction price | How to split consideration among promises? |
| **5** | Recognize revenue | When does customer obtain control? |

### Sequential Nature

Steps must be completed **in order** - conclusions at early steps affect later steps:
- No contract (Step 1) → Can't proceed
- # of POs (Step 2) → Affects allocation (Step 4)
- Transaction price (Step 3) → Amount allocated (Step 4)
- Allocated amounts (Step 4) → Revenue recognized (Step 5)

## Step 1: Identify the Contract with a Customer

### Five Contract Criteria (ALL must be met)

#### 1. Approval and Commitment
**Requirement**: All parties approved the contract and are committed to perform

**Evidence**:
- Written, oral, or implied by customary business practices
- Look for mutual assent

**CPA Exam Tip**: Don't assume contract must be written - oral and implied contracts qualify

#### 2. Rights Are Identifiable
**Requirement**: Can identify what goods/services each party is entitled to receive/provide

**Test**: Do both parties understand what is being bought/sold?

#### 3. Payment Terms Are Identifiable
**Requirement**: Can identify how payment will be calculated and when due

**Note**: Variable pricing is acceptable - just need framework for determination

**Examples**:
- ✓ "$100,000 due within 30 days"
- ✓ "$50/hour for time worked, invoiced monthly"
- ✗ "Price to be negotiated in good faith upon completion"

#### 4. Commercial Substance
**Requirement**: Contract expected to change entity's future cash flows (risk, timing, or amount)

**Purpose**: Excludes sham transactions

**CPA Exam Tip**: Rarely the issue in exam questions - most normal business contracts meet this

#### 5. Collection is Probable
**Requirement**: **Probable** (>50%) that entity will collect **substantially all** consideration

**Key Nuances**:
- "Substantially all" = amount entity is **entitled** to (not necessarily stated price)
- If entity expects to accept less due to price concession, assess collectibility of reduced amount
- Consider customer's ability AND intention to pay

**CPA Exam Tip**: This is often the "trick" criterion - questionable creditworthiness may fail this test

**Examples**:
- ✓ Established customer with strong credit history
- ✗ Customer in bankruptcy proceedings
- ? New customer with weak financials but entity has right to reclaim goods

### When Criteria Not Met

**If contract doesn't meet criteria**:
- Do NOT recognize revenue
- Recognize consideration received as **liability** (deposit)
- **Wait until**: (a) Criteria subsequently met, OR (b) No remaining obligations and received substantially all consideration

**Reassess**: At each reporting date if facts change

### Contract Combinations

**Combine multiple contracts if ANY of**:
- Negotiated as package with single commercial objective
- Consideration in one depends on the other
- Goods/services are single performance obligation

**Example**: Two contracts signed same day - one has below-market price, other has above-market price → Likely negotiated as package → Combine

## Step 2: Identify the Performance Obligations

### Definition

**Performance Obligation (PO)**: Promise to transfer good or service (or bundle) that is **distinct**

### What Counts as Promised?

**Explicit promises**: Stated in contract

**Implicit promises**: Implied by:
- Customary business practices
- Published policies
- Specific statements

**Excluded**:
- Activities that don't transfer goods/services to customer
- Immaterial promises

### Distinct Determination - BOTH Criteria Required

#### Criterion 1: Capable of Being Distinct

**Test**: Can customer benefit from the good/service either:
- On its own, OR
- Together with **readily available resources**?

**Readily Available Resources**:
- Goods/services customer already has
- Goods/services from other entities
- Customer's own resources

**Indicators**:
- Entity sells the item separately
- Customer could resell it
- Customer could use it with other readily available resources

**Examples**:
- ✓ Software license (can be installed and run)
- ✗ Specialized component that only works in larger system

#### Criterion 2: Distinct Within Context of Contract

**Test**: Is the promise **separately identifiable** from other promises?

**NOT separately identifiable if**:

**1. Significant integration service**:
- Entity integrates goods/services into combined output
- Example: Design + engineering + construction → Completed building

**2. Significant modification or customization**:
- One item significantly modifies another
- Example: Software customization that significantly modifies the software

**3. Highly interdependent or interrelated**:
- Can't fulfill one promise without the other
- Example: Specialized equipment designed for specific installation

**CPA Exam Tip**: Ask "What is customer really contracting for - multiple separate things or one integrated thing?"

### Both Criteria Required

**Decision Tree**:
1. Capable of being distinct? If NO → Bundle with others
2. If YES, distinct within context? If NO → Bundle with others
3. If YES to both → **Separate PO**

### Common Scenarios

**Equipment + Installation**:
- Standard equipment + routine installation → **TWO POs**
- Custom equipment + complex installation that adapts equipment → **ONE PO**

**Product + Warranty**:
- **Assurance-type** (assures compliance) → **NOT separate PO** (ASC 460)
  - Required by law, can't buy separately, covers defects at sale
- **Service-type** (additional service) → **SEPARATE PO**
  - Can buy separately, provides extra services, covers post-sale issues

**License + Services**:
- Off-the-shelf software + training → **TWO POs**
- IP license + significant customization → **ONE PO**

### Series of Distinct Goods/Services

**Practical expedient**: Series of substantially the same distinct goods/services with same pattern of transfer can be **single PO**

**Examples**:
- Daily cleaning services for one year → Single PO
- Monthly payroll processing → Single PO

## Step 3: Determine the Transaction Price

### Definition

**Transaction Price**: Amount of consideration entity **expects to be entitled to** in exchange for transferring goods/services (excluding amounts collected on behalf of third parties)

### Components

```
Transaction Price = Fixed Consideration
                  + Variable Consideration (estimated and constrained)
                  ± Time Value Adjustments
                  + Noncash Consideration
                  - Consideration Payable to Customer
```

### Variable Consideration

**Types**: Discounts, rebates, refunds, credits, price concessions, incentives, bonuses, penalties

**Estimation Required**: Use either:
- **Expected value** (probability-weighted), OR
- **Most likely amount**

**Constraint**: Include only to extent **probable** that significant reversal will NOT occur

### Significant Financing Component

**When exists**: Timing of payments provides customer or entity with significant financing benefit

**Adjustment**: Adjust for time value of money using appropriate discount rate

**Practical Expedient**: Don't adjust if period ≤ 1 year

**Direction**:
- Customer pays **after** transfer → **Decrease** transaction price (entity financing customer)
- Customer pays **before** transfer → **Increase** transaction price (customer financing entity)

### Noncash Consideration

**Measurement**: At **fair value**

**Examples**: Stock, goods/services received, cryptocurrency

### Consideration Payable to Customer

**Definition**: Cash entity pays to customer (or customer's customers)

**Examples**: Coupons, credits, slotting fees, co-op advertising

**Accounting**: **Reduce transaction price** (contra-revenue)
- UNLESS payment is for distinct good/service from customer

## Step 4: Allocate the Transaction Price

### Objective

Allocate transaction price to each PO in amount that depicts consideration entity expects to be entitled to for transferring that good/service

### Allocation Principle

Allocate based on **relative standalone selling prices (SSP)**

### Formula

```
Allocated Amount = (SSP of PO / Total SSP of All POs) × Transaction Price
```

### Standalone Selling Price (SSP)

**Definition**: Price at which entity would sell promised good/service **separately**

**Best Evidence**: Observable price when entity sells separately

**If Not Observable**: Estimate using:
- Adjusted market assessment
- Expected cost plus margin
- Residual approach (restricted use)

### Example

**Facts**:
- Contract for Product A + Service B
- Transaction price: $1,000
- Product A SSP: $600
- Service B SSP: $400
- Total SSP: $1,000

**Allocation**:
- Product A: ($600 / $1,000) × $1,000 = $600
- Service B: ($400 / $1,000) × $1,000 = $400

### With Discount Example

**Facts**:
- Same as above, but transaction price is $900 (10% discount)

**Allocation** (proportionate):
- Product A: ($600 / $1,000) × $900 = $540
- Service B: ($400 / $1,000) × $900 = $360

**Result**: Discount of $100 allocated proportionately

### Changes After Contract Inception

**Subsequent changes** to transaction price: Allocate on **same basis** as initial allocation

**Exception**: If change relates entirely to specific PO, allocate only to that PO

## Step 5: Recognize Revenue

### Satisfaction Principle

Entity satisfies PO by **transferring control** of promised good/service to customer

### Control Definition

**Control**: Ability to direct the use of, and obtain substantially all remaining benefits from, the asset

**Indicators**:
- Entity has present right to payment
- Customer has legal title
- Entity transferred physical possession
- Customer has significant risks/rewards
- Customer has accepted the asset

### Timing Determination

**Question**: Does PO meet criteria for **over-time** recognition?

- **If YES** → Recognize revenue **OVER TIME**
- **If NO** → Recognize revenue at **POINT IN TIME**

### Over-Time Criteria (ANY ONE sufficient)

**1. Customer simultaneously receives and consumes benefits** as entity performs
- Example: Routine cleaning services

**2. Entity's performance creates/enhances asset** that customer **controls** as created
- Example: Building on customer's land

**3. Entity's performance creates no asset with alternative use** to entity AND entity has **enforceable right to payment** for performance to date
- Example: Custom manufacturing with no alternative use

### Measuring Progress (Over Time)

**Methods**:

**Output methods**:
- Based on value transferred to customer
- Examples: Surveys, appraisals, milestones, units produced

**Input methods**:
- Based on entity's efforts or inputs
- Examples: Costs incurred, labor hours, time elapsed

**Choose method that best depicts performance**

### Point in Time Recognition

**When**: PO doesn't meet over-time criteria

**Determination**: Assess indicators of control transfer to identify point when control passes

## Interdependencies Between Steps

- **Step 1 → Step 2**: Contract scope defines promises analyzed
- **Step 2 → Step 4**: # of POs determines allocation
- **Step 3 → Step 4**: Transaction price is amount allocated
- **Step 4 → Step 5**: Allocated amounts = revenue recognized
- **Step 2 → Step 5**: Nature of PO affects timing

## Reassessment Requirements

**Each Reporting Period**:
- Contract criteria (if facts change significantly)
- Transaction price (variable consideration estimates)
- Progress toward completion (over-time POs)

**Generally No Reassessment**:
- Performance obligations (unless contract modified)

## CPA Exam Tips

### Multiple Choice Approach

1. **Identify which step** is being tested
2. **Step 1 keywords**: "collectibility," "contract," "enforceable"
3. **Step 2 keywords**: "distinct," "separately identifiable"
4. **Step 3 keywords**: "variable consideration," "constraint"
5. **Step 4 keywords**: "allocate," "standalone selling price"
6. **Step 5 keywords**: "when to recognize," "control"

### Common Distractors

- Confusing "capable of being distinct" with "distinct in context" (both required!)
- Assuming all variable consideration is included (must apply constraint)
- Allocating evenly instead of using relative SSP
- Assuming service contracts are always over time (must test criteria)
- Recognizing revenue when billed instead of when control transfers

### Memorization Aids

**Five Steps**: "I Identify Performance obligations, Determine price, Allocate, Recognize"

**Step 1 Criteria**: "All Parties Receive Payment with Commercial substance, Probably collected"

**Step 2 Distinct**: "Capable AND Context" (both required)

**Step 5 Over Time**: "Consumes, Controls creation, or Can't use elsewhere"

## Summary

### Key Takeaways

1. **Five-step model** provides systematic framework
2. **Steps are sequential** - each affects subsequent steps
3. **Step 1**: All 5 criteria must be met - **collectibility** often the issue
4. **Step 2**: **Both** distinct criteria required - focus on **integration** and **interdependence**
5. **Step 3**: Estimate and **constrain** variable consideration
6. **Step 4**: Allocate based on **relative SSP** (proportionate)
7. **Step 5**: Test **over-time** criteria first; if none met, recognize at **point in time**
8. **Reassess estimates** each reporting period

### Quick Decision Framework

**Step 1**: Do all 5 criteria exist? → If NO, no contract (stop)

**Step 2**: For each promise, ask:
- Capable of being distinct? AND
- Distinct in context?
→ If YES to both, separate PO

**Step 3**: Fixed + Variable (constrained) ± Financing + Noncash - Payable to customer = Transaction Price

**Step 4**: Allocate using (SSP of PO / Total SSP) × Transaction Price

**Step 5**: Over-time (any 1 criterion)? → YES = Over time, NO = Point in time
