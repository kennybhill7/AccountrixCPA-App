# Risks and Uncertainties Disclosures - ASC 275

## Overview
ASC 275 requires disclosures about risks and uncertainties existing at the financial statement date that could significantly affect amounts reported in the near term.

## Four Areas of Required Disclosure

### 1. Nature of Operations
**Required**: Describe major products/services and principal markets

**Example**: "Company manufactures automotive parts, primarily for the North American market."

### 2. Use of Estimates
**Required**: Statement that preparation of financial statements requires estimates that may differ from actual results

**Standard Language**: "The preparation of financial statements in conformity with GAAP requires management to make estimates and assumptions that affect reported amounts of assets, liabilities, revenues, and expenses. Actual results could differ from those estimates."

**Important**: This disclosure is **required for ALL GAAP financial statements**.

### 3. Certain Significant Estimates
**Required if BOTH criteria met**:
1. It is reasonably possible the estimate will change materially in the near term
2. Effect of change would be material to financial statements

**Disclosure includes**:
- Nature of uncertainty
- Indication that reasonably possible change could occur
- **Not required** to quantify the amount

**Examples**:
- Litigation outcome uncertain
- Warranty liability depends on future claims
- Collectibility of major receivable uncertain
- Environmental remediation costs
- Long-term contract revenues
- Pension obligations

### 4. Current Vulnerability to Concentrations
**Required when**:
- Concentration exists
- Vulnerability to near-term severe impact is reasonably possible
- Concentration not already disclosed elsewhere

**Types of Concentrations**:

#### Revenue Concentration
Single customer or group representing >10% of revenue

**Example**: "Company derives approximately 35% of revenues from a single customer. Loss of this customer would have a material adverse effect."

#### Supplier Concentration
Single supplier providing critical input

**Example**: "Company relies on a single supplier for a critical component. Disruption in supply could temporarily halt production."

#### Geographic Concentration
Operations in single region subject to risks (political, economic, natural disasters)

#### Product Concentration
Heavy reliance on single product line

**Disclosure Content**:
- Nature of concentration
- Extent of concentration (e.g., percentage)
- Statement that near-term severe impact is reasonably possible

## Key Definitions

### Near Term
Period of time **not to exceed one year** from the financial statement date.

### Reasonably Possible
More than remote but less than probable.
- Approximate probability: >5% but <75%
- More likely than "remote" but less likely than "probable"

### Severe Impact
Significant financially disruptive effect to normal functioning of the entity:
- Bankruptcy
- Forced sale of assets
- Major restructuring
- Inability to continue operations

## Examples of Disclosures

### Concentration Example
"The Company's sales to its three largest customers represented 55% of total revenues. The loss of any of these customers could materially impact operations."

### Estimate Example
"The Company is a defendant in litigation. Management cannot reasonably estimate the outcome, but an adverse decision could have a material effect on the Company's financial position and results of operations."

### Supplier Example
"The Company relies on a single supplier for a critical component. A disruption in supply could temporarily halt production."

## What is NOT Required

- **Do not need to predict future events**
- **Do not need to quantify potential losses**
- Just disclose existence and nature of risk

## Going Concern Assessment

### Relationship to ASC 275
- Going concern is addressed in **ASC 205-40** (related but separate topic)
- Evaluate whether substantial doubt exists about ability to continue as going concern for one year from financial statement issuance date

### If Substantial Doubt Exists
Disclose:
1. Principal conditions/events
2. Management's evaluation
3. Management's plans

## CPA Exam Tips

1. **Four areas of disclosure**:
   - Nature of operations
   - Use of estimates (always required)
   - Certain significant estimates (if criteria met)
   - Concentrations (if vulnerable)

2. **Use of estimates**: Required for **ALL GAAP financial statements**

3. **Significant estimates**: Disclose if:
   - Reasonably possible change in near term **AND**
   - Material effect

4. **Concentrations**: Disclose if vulnerability to near-term severe impact is reasonably possible

5. **Near term** = One year or less

6. **Don't need to quantify** potential losses, just describe nature of risk

## Summary Table

| Disclosure Area | When Required |
|----------------|---------------|
| Nature of operations | Always |
| Use of estimates | Always (for all GAAP statements) |
| Significant estimates | If reasonably possible change + material effect |
| Concentrations | If vulnerability to severe impact reasonably possible |

## Key Takeaways

- Four disclosure areas: Nature of operations, Use of estimates, Significant estimates, Concentrations
- Use of estimates disclosure is **required for ALL GAAP financial statements**
- Significant estimates: Disclose if reasonably possible change AND material effect
- Concentrations: Disclose if vulnerability to near-term severe impact is reasonably possible
- Near term = One year or less from financial statement date
- Not required to quantify potential losses or predict outcomes
