# Performance Obligations - Identification and Analysis Under ASC 606

## Overview

**Definition**: A promise in a contract to transfer to the customer either:
- **(a)** A good or service (or bundle) that is **distinct**, OR
- **(b)** A series of distinct goods or services that are **substantially the same** with the **same pattern of transfer**

**Key Principle**: The unit of account for revenue recognition is the **performance obligation**, not the contract or individual items

**Importance**: PO identification is Step 2 of five-step model and is critical because it:
- Determines how transaction price is allocated (Step 4)
- Affects timing of revenue recognition (Step 5)
- Impacts financial statement presentation
- Requires significant judgment
- Heavily tested on CPA exam

## Promises in Contracts

### Types of Promises

**1. Explicit Promises**:
- Goods or services explicitly stated in contract
- Examples: Product delivery in purchase order, services in statement of work

**2. Implicit Promises**:
- Goods or services implied by entity's customary practices, published policies, or specific statements
- Examples:
  - Free shipping if website states "all orders ship free"
  - Free technical support if entity always provides it
  - Customary setup activities customers expect
  - Installation routinely provided without separate charge
- **Key Point**: Past practices create implicit promises even if not written

### Activities That Are NOT Promises

Not all activities transfer goods or services to customer:

| Activity | Examples | Rationale |
|----------|----------|-----------|
| **Administrative tasks** | Setting up internal systems, obtaining approvals, allocating resources | Internal activities that don't transfer value to customer |
| **Setup activities** | Mobilizing workforce, initial planning benefiting entity | Don't result in transfer of good/service |

**Exception**: If setup creates asset customer controls, may be part of PO

### Immaterial Promises

**Principle**: Need not assess if promises are immaterial in context of the contract

**Key Point**: Materiality assessed at **contract level**, not entity level

**Examples**: Free promotional item of nominal value, incidental services

**CPA Exam Tip**: If question doesn't emphasize item or describes as "incidental," treat as immaterial

## Distinct Criteria - The Two-Part Test

### Overview

**A good or service is DISTINCT if BOTH criteria are met**:
1. Capable of being distinct (Criterion 1)
2. Distinct within context of contract (Criterion 2)

**Meeting only one criterion is insufficient - BOTH required**

## Criterion 1: Capable of Being Distinct

### Definition

**The customer can benefit from the good or service either on its own OR together with other resources that are readily available to the customer**

### Key Test

Can customer obtain economic benefit from the item:
- On its own, OR
- Together with resources customer already has or can easily obtain?

### Readily Available Resources

Resources that are:
- Sold separately by entity or another entity
- Already obtained by customer from entity (including under this contract)
- Resources customer could obtain from another source

**Note**: Customer need not actually use resources; they just need to be available

### Indicators Criterion 1 MET

| Indicator | Strength | Explanation | Example |
|-----------|----------|-------------|---------|
| **Entity regularly sells separately** | Strong | Demonstrates customer can benefit | Software vendor sells licenses without installation |
| **Customer could resell** | Strong | Resale demonstrates standalone value | Equipment customer could sell |
| **Use with readily available items** | Moderate | Even if needs additional items, if available, criterion met | Specialized software requiring standard hardware |

### Indicators Criterion 1 NOT MET

- Item only functions with other specific items in contract (no alternatives)
- Highly specialized with no alternative use
- No resale value and can't be used alone

### Examples

**Standard laptop with routine setup**:
```
Analysis: Laptop is capable of being distinct
  - Customer could use laptop alone
  - Or hire any technician to set it up (readily available)
Conclusion: Criterion 1 MET ✓
```

**Highly specialized component**:
```
Analysis: Component only functions when integrated into customer's
          unique system, no other suppliers can provide integration
Conclusion: Criterion 1 NOT MET ✗
```

**Software license (functional on its own)**:
```
Analysis: Customer can use software alone or obtain
          customization from other vendors
Conclusion: Criterion 1 MET ✓
```

**Proprietary drug formula**:
```
Analysis: Formula has no standalone benefit
          (must be manufactured into finished drug)
Conclusion: Criterion 1 NOT MET ✗
```

### CPA Exam Tips for Criterion 1

**Look for clue words**:
- "Sold separately" → Likely met
- "Could hire another provider" → Likely met
- "Standard item" → Likely met
- "Functional on its own" → Likely met
- "Has no standalone value" → Likely NOT met
- "Can't function without" → Likely NOT met

**Key points**:
- If entity or competitors sell separately, typically met
- Focus on customer's **ability** to benefit, not what they **plan** to do

## Criterion 2: Distinct Within the Context of the Contract

### Definition

**The promise to transfer the good or service is separately identifiable from other promises in the contract**

### Key Test

Is the good or service:
- **Highly interdependent** OR
- **Highly interrelated**

...with other goods/services such that they function as **inputs to a combined output**?

### Principle

This criterion focuses on **relationship between promises**:
- Are items **separate outputs**? OR
- Are they **inputs to produce combined, integrated output**?

### Three-Factor Framework

To assess whether promises are separately identifiable:

#### Factor 1: Significant Integration Service

**Question**: Is entity providing a significant service of integrating goods or services?

**Description**: Entity uses items as inputs to produce or deliver a **combined output** specified by customer

**If YES** → NOT separately identifiable (fails Criterion 2)

**Examples**:
- General contractor integrates design, materials, construction to produce completed building
- Systems integrator combines hardware, software, integration to deliver functioning IT system
- Manufacturer combines materials, labor, process to deliver finished product

**Key Question**: Is entity creating **one unified output** or delivering **multiple separate outputs**?

#### Factor 2: Significant Modification or Customization

**Question**: Does one or more goods/services significantly modify or customize others?

**Description**: Items significantly affect or transform other items in contract

**If YES** → NOT separately identifiable (fails Criterion 2)

**Examples**:
- Customization services that significantly modify software for customer's unique requirements
- Engineering that customizes equipment design for customer's specific facility
- Installation that modifies equipment to integrate with customer's systems

**Key Question**: Does one item significantly **change or transform** the other(s)?

#### Factor 3: Highly Interdependent or Interrelated

**Question**: Are goods or services highly interdependent or highly interrelated?

**Description**: Entity cannot fulfill promise to transfer one item without the other(s) - items are dependent

**If YES** → NOT separately identifiable (fails Criterion 2)

**Examples**:
- Equipment designed for customer that cannot function without specialized installation only entity can provide
- Software components that must work together to deliver promised functionality

**Key Question**: Could entity deliver one item without the other? Are risks intertwined?

### When Separately Identifiable (Criterion 2 Met)

Promises are separately identifiable if:
- Entity transferring **multiple distinct outputs**
- Items **don't significantly affect** each other
- **Risks of transferring** one item are separate from others
- Entity could **fulfill promise independently**

## Detailed Examples - Both Criteria

### Example 1: Standard Laptop + Routine Setup

**Promises**: Laptop and setup (loading software, configuring email)

**Criterion 1 - Capable**:
```
✓ Customer could use laptop without entity's setup
✓ Customer could hire anyone to perform setup
Conclusion: Criterion 1 MET
```

**Criterion 2 - Separately Identifiable**:
```
Significant integration? NO - Setup doesn't integrate into combined output
Significant modification? NO - Setup doesn't modify laptop hardware/software
Highly interdependent? NO - Entity could deliver laptop without setup
Conclusion: Criterion 2 MET
```

**Result**: **TWO performance obligations** (Laptop, Setup)

### Example 2: Custom Equipment + Specialized Installation

**Promises**: Custom manufacturing equipment and specialized installation that integrates into customer's production line

**Criterion 1 - Capable**:
```
✓ Equipment could theoretically be resold
Conclusion: Criterion 1 MET
```

**Criterion 2 - Separately Identifiable**:
```
Significant integration? YES - Combining equipment and installation to
                             deliver integrated production line component
Significant modification? YES - Installation customizes equipment for
                               specific line
Highly interdependent? YES - Equipment designed for this specific
                            installation
Conclusion: Criterion 2 NOT MET ✗
```

**Result**: **ONE performance obligation** (Equipment with Installation)

### Example 3: Design and Construction of Building

**Promises**: Design and construction of custom office building

**Criterion 1 - Capable**:
```
✓ Design documents have standalone value
✓ Construction services have standalone value
Conclusion: Criterion 1 MET for both
```

**Criterion 2 - Separately Identifiable**:
```
Significant integration? YES - Integrating design and construction to
                             deliver completed building
Combined output? YES - Customer contracted for ONE building, not
                      separate design and construction
Interdependent? YES - Design specifically for this construction;
                     construction follows this design
Conclusion: Criterion 2 NOT MET ✗
```

**Result**: **ONE performance obligation** (Integrated project)

### Example 4: Off-the-Shelf Software + Training

**Promises**: Software license and training on how to use software

**Criterion 1 - Capable**:
```
✓ Software is functional without training
✓ Training could be obtained elsewhere
Conclusion: Criterion 1 MET for both
```

**Criterion 2 - Separately Identifiable**:
```
Significant integration? NO - Not integrating into combined output
Significant modification? NO - Training doesn't modify software
Highly interdependent? NO - Software works without training;
                           training available elsewhere
Conclusion: Criterion 2 MET ✓
```

**Result**: **TWO performance obligations** (Software, Training)

### Example 5: Proprietary Software + Essential Customization

**Promises**: Proprietary software and essential customization to make it work for customer's business

**Criterion 1 - Capable**:
```
Software alone? Has some value but won't provide promised functionality
Customization alone? No value without software
Conclusion: Debatable, but assume Criterion 1 MET
```

**Criterion 2 - Separately Identifiable**:
```
Significant integration? YES - Combining software and customization to
                             deliver functioning business solution
Significant modification? YES - Customization significantly modifies
                               software
Interdependent? YES - Software won't provide promised functionality
                     without customization
Conclusion: Criterion 2 NOT MET ✗
```

**Result**: **ONE performance obligation** (Software with Customization)

## Decision Matrix - Both Criteria Required

| Criterion 1 | Criterion 2 | Result | Example |
|-------------|-------------|--------|---------|
| ✓ Met | ✓ Met | **DISTINCT** → Separate PO | Standard equipment + routine installation |
| ✓ Met | ✗ NOT Met | **NOT DISTINCT** → Bundle | Equipment + highly customized installation |
| ✗ NOT Met | ✓ Met | **NOT DISTINCT** → Bundle | Specialized component even if not integrated |
| ✗ NOT Met | ✗ NOT Met | **NOT DISTINCT** → Bundle | Specialized component highly integrated |

## Bundling Analysis

### When Items Not Distinct

**Principle**: If promised good or service is not distinct, **combine** it with other promises until you identify a bundle that is distinct

**Process**:

**Step 1**: Identify all promised goods and services

**Step 2**: Test each for both distinct criteria

**Step 3**: For items that are not distinct, bundle them

**Step 4**: Test whether the bundle is distinct

**Step 5**: Continue bundling until distinct bundle(s) identified

### Example - Complex IT System

**Promised Items**:
- Hardware (Server 1, Server 2)
- Software (License A, License B)
- Configuration services
- Integration services
- Testing
- Training

**Analysis**:

**Step 1 - Test Criterion 1**:
```
Hardware capable? YES (could be resold)
Software capable? YES (has functionality)
Services capable? YES (could hire others)
All meet Criterion 1 ✓
```

**Step 2 - Test Criterion 2**:
```
Is entity integrating hardware, software, services into functioning system? YES
Are items significantly interdependent? YES (each configured for integrated system)
Criterion 2 FAILS for hardware, software, configuration, integration, testing ✗
```

**Step 3 - Bundle those items**:
```
Bundle: All hardware + all software + configuration + integration + testing
       = Integrated IT System

Test if bundle is distinct:
  Capable? YES - Functioning system has standalone value
  Separately identifiable? YES - The system is the output
Bundle is distinct ✓
```

**Step 4 - Training**:
```
Separate from system? YES - Training doesn't modify system
Training is distinct? YES - Both criteria met ✓
```

**Result**: **TWO performance obligations**
1. Integrated IT System
2. Training

## Series Provision

### Definition

A **series** of distinct goods or services that are **substantially the same** and have the **same pattern of transfer**

### Treatment

Account for series as **SINGLE performance obligation** if:
1. Each distinct item meets criteria to be satisfied **over time**
2. Same method would be used to **measure progress**

### Benefits

- **Practical expedient**: Treats repetitive distinct services as one PO instead of hundreds/thousands
- **Simplification**: Same revenue result but simpler
- **Examples**:
  - Daily cleaning services for 1 year = 1 PO (not 365)
  - Monthly payroll processing = 1 PO (not 12)
  - Weekly newspaper delivery = 1 PO (not 52)

### Example - Security Guard Services

**Scenario**: Provide security guard at facility 24/7 for one year

**Analysis**:
```
Each hour distinct? YES (customer receives benefit as performed)
Over-time criterion? YES (customer simultaneously receives and consumes)
Same progress measure? YES (time-based - recognize ratably)
```

**Conclusion**: Treat as **SINGLE performance obligation** (series)
- Recognize revenue ratably over year
- Alternative would be 8,760 POs (one per hour) with same result
- Series provision is practical shortcut

### When NOT to Use Series Provision

| Situation | Example |
|-----------|---------|
| **Services not substantially same** | Consulting with different services each month (strategy, implementation, training) |
| **Different progress measures** | Mix of point-in-time and over-time deliveries |
| **Services not over time** | Monthly product deliveries (point in time) |

## Common CPA Exam Scenarios

### Equipment + Installation

**Key Variables**:
- Is equipment standard or custom?
- Is installation routine or specialized?
- Does installation modify/customize equipment?
- Could customer hire someone else?

**Typical Outcomes**:

**Standard equipment + routine installation**:
```
Analysis: Equipment capable (resalable), installation routine
          (anyone could do it), no significant integration
Result: TWO POs (Equipment, Installation)
```

**Custom equipment + specialized installation**:
```
Analysis: Installation integrates into customer's system or
          significantly customizes, highly interdependent
Result: ONE PO (Equipment with Installation)
```

**CPA Exam Tip**: Look for words:
- "Specialized," "customized," "integrated," "adapted" → Likely ONE PO
- "Standard," "routine," "basic" → Likely TWO POs

### Software and Services

**Service Types** and typical treatment:

| Service | Typical Result |
|---------|---------------|
| **Training** | Separate PO - doesn't modify software, not integrated, available elsewhere |
| **Basic implementation** | Depends - if routine configuration → separate. If complex customization → one PO |
| **Significant customization** | ONE PO - significantly modifies software, creating combined output |
| **Ongoing support** | Separate PO - support period distinct from license and implementation |

### Construction Contracts

**Typical Analysis**: Design, engineering, and construction typically **NOT separately identifiable**
- Entity provides significant integration service
- Customer contracts for **one building**, not separate design and construction

**Result**: **ONE PO** (Integrated project)

**Exception**: If design complete and approved before construction contract, and construction could be done by another party, may be separate POs

**CPA Exam Tip**: Construction projects almost always ONE PO unless facts clearly indicate separate contracts/phases

### Product with Warranty

**Analysis depends on warranty type**:

| Warranty Type | Description | Result |
|---------------|-------------|--------|
| **Assurance-type** | Warranty that product complies with specifications | NOT a PO (account for as loss contingency) |
| | Indicators: Required by law, can't be purchased separately, covers defects at sale |
| **Service-type** | Provides service beyond assurance | SEPARATE PO |
| | Indicators: Can purchase separately, additional coverage, covers issues after sale |

### Licensing Scenarios

**Key Question**: Does license have significant standalone functionality?

**Functional IP** (software, drug formula, completed media):
- Standalone functionality: YES
- Typical result: License is separate PO if services don't significantly modify
- Example: Software license + training = TWO POs

**Symbolic IP** (brand, franchise, trademark):
- Standalone functionality: NO - value derives from entity's ongoing activities
- Typical result: License combined with ongoing support may be ONE PO
- Example: Franchise license + ongoing support = May be ONE PO if highly integrated

## Practical Application Process

### Step-by-Step

**Step 1: List all promises**
- Action: Identify everything entity promised (explicit and implicit)
- Output: Complete list of promised goods and services

**Step 2: Exclude non-promises**
- Action: Remove administrative tasks, setup not transferring value, immaterial items
- Output: Refined list to evaluate

**Step 3: Test Criterion 1 for each**
- Action: Can customer benefit alone or with readily available resources?
- Output: List showing which meet Criterion 1

**Step 4: Test Criterion 2 for items that met Criterion 1**
- Action: Separately identifiable? (Integration? Modification? Interdependence?)
- Output: List showing which meet both criteria (distinct)

**Step 5: Bundle non-distinct items**
- Action: Combine items that failed either criterion until bundle is distinct
- Output: Distinct bundles

**Step 6: Consider series provision**
- Action: If repetitive distinct services with same pattern, consider single PO
- Output: Final list of performance obligations

## CPA Exam Strategies

### Recognition Signals

**ONE Performance Obligation Likely**:
- Construction/engineering project
- Custom manufacturing with specialized installation
- Significant integration of multiple components
- Customization that significantly modifies items
- Design-build contracts
- Systems integration projects
- **Words**: "integrated," "customized," "specialized," "combined output"

**MULTIPLE Performance Obligations Likely**:
- Standard products with standard services
- Training separate from products/software
- Warranty that can be purchased separately
- Products that could be resold
- Services obtainable from other providers
- **Words**: "standard," "routine," "off-the-shelf," "could be purchased separately"

### Systematic Approach

1. Always test **BOTH** distinct criteria - don't assume
2. Explicitly state your analysis for each criterion
3. Use three-factor framework for Criterion 2 (integration, modification, interdependence)
4. When in doubt, lean toward bundling (fewer POs) for integrated projects
5. Show your work - partial credit available
6. State conclusion clearly: "X performance obligations: (1)..., (2)..."

### Common Errors

- Testing only one criterion instead of both
- Confusing "capable of being distinct" with "distinct in context"
- Assuming everything listed separately is separate PO
- Forgetting implicit promises can create POs
- Not considering if entity provides integration service
- Overthinking simple scenarios

### Time Management

- PO identification can be time-consuming in simulations
- Simple scenarios (standard laptop + basic setup) → Don't overthink, likely two POs
- Complex scenarios (design-build project) → Expect one PO, focus on Criterion 2
- Create simple chart listing items and whether each criterion met

## Summary

### Critical Points

- **Performance obligation** = distinct good or service (or bundle)
- **Distinct requires BOTH**: (1) capable of being distinct, AND (2) distinct in context
- **Criterion 1**: Can customer benefit alone or with readily available resources?
- **Criterion 2**: Separately identifiable? (Test: integration, modification, interdependence)
- If not distinct, **bundle** until distinct
- **Construction/integration** projects typically have few POs (often one)
- **Standard products/services** typically have multiple POs
- **Series provision**: Repetitive distinct services over time with same progress measure = single PO
- **Most heavily tested** Step 2 concept on CPA exam
