# Special Purpose Financial Reporting Frameworks

## Overview
Special purpose frameworks are financial reporting frameworks designed to meet the needs of specific users, rather than general purpose frameworks like GAAP.

## Types of Special Purpose Frameworks

### OCBOA (Other Comprehensive Basis of Accounting)
- **Cash basis**: Recognize revenue when cash received, expenses when cash paid
- **Tax basis**: Follow income tax accounting rules
- **Regulatory basis**: Follow regulatory agency requirements (insurance statutory, bank regulatory)
- **Contractual basis**: Follow specific contractual agreements

### Incomplete GAAP Presentations
- **Definition**: One or more financial statements prepared in accordance with GAAP, but not a complete set
- **Examples**:
  - Balance sheet only
  - Income statement only
  - Single element (e.g., schedule of revenue)
- **When used**: Contractual agreements, regulatory filings, specific user needs
- **Disclosure**: Must disclose incomplete presentation and basis of accounting (GAAP)

## When Special Purpose Frameworks are Appropriate

1. **Private companies** (not required to use GAAP)
2. **Specific contractual requirements** (lender requires cash basis)
3. **Regulatory compliance** (insurance statutory, bank regulatory)
4. **Internal use only**
5. **Limited distribution** to specific users

## Financial Statement Titles

### GAAP Titles
- Balance Sheet
- Income Statement
- Statement of Cash Flows
- Statement of Stockholders' Equity

### Special Purpose Titles (must use different titles)
- Statement of Assets and Liabilities - Modified Cash Basis
- Statement of Revenue and Expenses - Income Tax Basis
- Statement of Assets, Liabilities, and Net Assets - Regulatory Basis

**Key Point**: Cannot use GAAP titles if not prepared under GAAP.

## Comparative Advantages

### GAAP
**Pros**:
- Comprehensive and comparable
- Accepted by lenders and investors
- Required for public companies

**Cons**:
- Complex and costly
- Requires significant expertise

### Special Purpose
**Pros**:
- Simpler and less costly
- Meets specific needs
- Flexible

**Cons**:
- Less comparable across entities
- Limited distribution
- Not acceptable for public companies

## Disclosure Requirements

1. **Basis of accounting**: Describe framework used (cash, tax, regulatory, etc.)
2. **Differences from GAAP**: Explain how framework differs from GAAP (qualitative description)
3. **Purpose**: State purpose of financial statements (if limited use)
4. **Consistency**: Disclose if framework changed from prior period
5. **Material transactions**: Disclose material transactions even if not typical for the framework

## Audit and Review Considerations

### Auditing Special Purpose Statements
- Auditor can audit special purpose financial statements
- **Report modifications**: Audit report states framework used, may restrict distribution
- **Emphasis-of-matter paragraph**: Highlights special purpose framework in use
- **Review or compilation**: Can also perform review or compilation on special purpose statements

### Distribution Restrictions
- **General use**: GAAP financial statements for general use
- **Restricted use**: Special purpose statements may be restricted to specific parties (management, lender, regulator)
- **Audit report**: Auditor may add restricting paragraph if appropriate

## Examples by Industry

- **Construction**: Percentage-of-completion or completed contract (now ASC 606 over time)
- **Insurance**: Statutory accounting (regulatory basis)
- **Government**: Fund accounting, GASB standards
- **Not-for-profit**: FASB standards for NFPs

## Transition from Special Purpose to GAAP

### Going Public
- Must convert to GAAP
- Restate prior periods for comparability

### New Lender Requirements
- May need to prepare GAAP statements alongside special purpose
- **Cost consideration**: Maintaining both sets can be costly

## Going Concern in Special Purpose Frameworks

- Going concern assessment **still applies** regardless of framework
- Disclose substantial doubt if it exists
- Same standards as GAAP (ASC 205-40)

## CPA Exam Tips

1. **Special purpose frameworks**: Designed for specific users, not general purpose
2. **Types**: OCBOA (cash, tax, regulatory, contractual), incomplete GAAP presentations
3. **Financial statement titles must reflect basis** (not use GAAP titles if not GAAP)
4. **Disclose**: Basis used, differences from GAAP, purpose of statements
5. **Audit report**: States framework, may restrict distribution
6. **Appropriate for**: Private companies, contractual requirements, regulatory compliance

## Key Takeaways

- Special purpose frameworks meet specific user needs, not general purpose like GAAP
- Types include OCBOA and incomplete GAAP presentations
- Financial statement titles must reflect the framework used
- Disclosure of basis, differences from GAAP, and purpose is required
- Audit reports will note the special purpose framework and may restrict distribution
- Appropriate for private companies with specific contractual or regulatory needs
